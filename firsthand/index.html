Firsthand From told to known

You can grow up in church, know the right answers, serve for years, and still only know God secondhand: through what someone else heard, preached, or told you to do.

This page is about the trade Israel made at the mountain, why so many of us still make it, and what walking with God looks like when it is your own.

Secondhand faith is a relay. Every hop keeps the instructions and loses the voice. By the time it reaches you, a love letter has become a to-do list.

God
“I carried you on eagles’ wings and brought you to myself.”
Moses
“The Lord says you are his treasured possession.”
The leaders
“We were told there are rules to keep.”
You
Do this. Don’t do that. Be there Sunday.

“You will be for me a kingdom of priests.” Not one man on the mountain. Everyone. The invitation was always to you.

1The trade at the mountain

They asked for a messenger instead of a meeting.

Moses knew God as a friend. He brought the whole nation out to meet him. They heard God’s voice, got scared, and sent Moses back up alone. Here is how it happened, in six frames.

01

The invitation

God’s goal was not a rulebook. It was “to myself,” and every person a priest with their own access.

02

The fear

God spoke the Ten Commandments to everyone directly. Then the people stepped back.

03

“You go”

This is the trade in their own words: you go near, you listen, then tell us what he wants.

04

The tent

Moses kept meeting God face to face. The people watched from their own doorways. Only young Joshua stayed in the tent.

05

Moses’ wish

Moses never wanted to be the only one. When others started hearing God, he was glad.

06

It happened again

Generations later, Israel asked Samuel for a king “like the other nations.” Another go-between. God named it plainly.

Be fair to Israel. God himself said their request was good (Deuteronomy 5:28), and he had set limits on the mountain too (Exodus 19:12). Fear of a holy God is not foolish. But listen to what God says right after: “Oh, that their hearts would be inclined to fear me.” The problem was never asking for help. It was settling for distance.

Jesus put his finger on the same thing more than a thousand years later. You can study every word God said and still never come to him: John 5:39-40.

2Told or known?

What secondhand faith looks like from the inside.

Tap anything that sounds like you. Nobody sees this but you, and it is a mirror, not a grade. Most people who love God find two or three.

Your mirror

Nothing tapped yet. The patterns below fill in as you go.

This is not about whether you are saved. It is about whether you are close.

The task list

God becomes a set of jobs. Busy for him, rarely with him.

Martha, Luke 10:41-42

The audience

Faith is shaped by who is watching. People’s opinion sets the rules.

The leaders in John 12:42-43

The borrowed voice

Everything God says comes through someone else. You obey instructions, not a voice.

Israel at Sinai, Exodus 20:19

The Sunday box

God lives in one room of the week. Lips near, heart far.

Isaiah 29:13

The wage earner

Living like a hired hand in your Father’s house. Earning what is already yours.

The older brother, Luke 15:29-31
3Obey means listen

In the Bible, obedience starts with an ear, not a list.

שָׁמַע
shamahearlistenunderstandobey

One Hebrew word does all four jobs.

When the Old Testament says “obey,” it very often uses shama, the ordinary word for hearing. In the New Testament the usual word is hypakouō, literally “to listen under.” Obedience, in both, is what happens when you actually hear someone you trust. It is a response to a voice.

  • Exodus 19:5“If you obey me fully” is, in Hebrew, “if you will truly listen to my voice.”
  • Deuteronomy 6:4Israel’s central confession begins with this word: “Hear, O Israel.”
  • 1 Samuel 15:22“To obey is better than sacrifice” is “to listen is better.”
  • 1 Samuel 3:10The boy Samuel: “Speak, for your servant is listening.”

This changes what a “disobedient” Christian usually is. Most of us are not rebels. We are people obeying instructions other people received, without ever hearing the voice they came from. That kind of obedience is brittle. It lasts as long as someone is watching or the rules make sense.

Jesus describes a different thing. Sheep do not follow a printed route. They follow a voice they have learned by being near it.

4Two fears

One fear sends you away. The other keeps you close.

In one breath Moses told the people “do not be afraid” and said God wanted them to fear him (Exodus 20:20). He was talking about two different things.

Fear that runs

“Do not have God speak to us or we will die.” This fear expects punishment, so it keeps its distance and hires someone to go instead. Adam had it first: “I was afraid, so I hid.” John says love drives it out (1 John 4:18).

Fear that stays

Awe. Taking God seriously because you are close enough to see who he is. This is “the fear of the Lord” that is the beginning of wisdom. It does not push you out of the tent. It keeps you in it, like Joshua.

Fear of man

When God feels far, people feel huge. Their opinion fills the space his voice should have. Some leaders in Jesus’ day believed in him and still kept quiet, because they wanted the room’s approval more.

An audience of one

Jesus’ answer to performing for people was not to try harder. It was a room with the door shut, where the only one watching is the one who loves you. Practice there is what makes you free in public.

5What relationship actually looks like

Seven verbs. None of them is “attend.”

Scripture describes life with God with ordinary words for being with someone. Before Sinai there were no commandments at all, and Enoch “walked faithfully with God.”

Walk

Side by side, at his pace, going somewhere together. Not a meeting you attend. A road you share.

Micah 6:8, Galatians 5:25

Listen

Leave room for him to speak. Prayer is a conversation, and conversations have two voices.

1 Samuel 3:10, John 10:27

Talk

All day, about anything. Not speeches. The running commentary you’d share with a friend in the car.

1 Thessalonians 5:17, Philippians 4:6

Abide

Stay connected, the way a branch does. Fruit is what grows from that, not what earns it.

John 15:4-5

Follow

Jesus rarely handed people a plan. He said “follow me” and told them the next step.

Mark 1:17, John 21:22

Rest

The invitation to the tired is not a better system. It is “come to me.” Yoked to him, the load is light.

Matthew 11:28-30

Be led

Children of God are the ones led by his Spirit. Led is a living word: it happens now, step by step.

Romans 8:14-16, Isaiah 30:21
6Hearing his voice

How God speaks, and how to know it was him.

Most people who say “God never speaks to me” are expecting thunder. Elijah learned that God was not in the wind, the earthquake or the fire. He was in a whisper (1 Kings 19:11-12).

Scripture, read as letters from a Person

The Bible is the main way he speaks, and the test for every other way. Read it to meet him, not just to learn about him.

A quiet inner nudge

A thought you didn’t produce, a verse that suddenly lands, a name that comes to mind. The Spirit “testifies with our spirit.”

Peace, acting as umpire

In Colossians 3:15 “rule” is the word for an umpire. His peace calls it, and its absence is a signal too.

Other believers

The early church decided things this way: “It seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us” (Acts 15:28). Others confirm. They don’t replace.

Doors that open and close

Paul tried to go into Asia and Bithynia and was stopped, then saw a vision of Macedonia (Acts 16:6-10). Circumstances can be part of the conversation.

Test it: five filters

John says to test what you hear (1 John 4:1). Real guidance gets through all five. Anything that fails one, set down.

  1. 1
    Does it agree with Scripture?God does not contradict what he has already said.
  2. 2
    Does it sound like Jesus?His tone, not only his words. Kind, clear, never crushing.
  3. 3
    Would it grow good fruit?Love, joy, peace, patience (Galatians 5:22-23).
  4. 4
    Does peace rule as you hold it?Not the absence of nerves. The umpire’s call.
  5. 5
    Do wise believers confirm it?Especially for big decisions. A matter is settled by two or three witnesses.
7Two starting points

Whether you’ve been in church for decades or a week.

The church in Ephesus had everything right: hard work, endurance, sound doctrine. Jesus still had one thing against them.

  1. RememberJesus’ first instruction to Ephesus: “Consider how far you have fallen” (Revelation 2:5). Go back to when it was real. What did you do then that you stopped doing?
  2. Stop one thing for a weekPick one religious task you do on autopilot and swap it for ten unhurried minutes with him. No agenda. Notice what you feel.
  3. Read like the older brother finally heard it“You are always with me, and everything I have is yours.” You are not a long-serving employee. You are a son or a daughter who forgot.
  4. Say his names with the “my”Many of God’s names in Hebrew carry a pronoun: the Lord is my shepherd (Psalm 23:1), my banner (Exodus 17:15), the Lord who heals you (Exodus 15:26), the Lord our righteousness (Jeremiah 23:6). Secondhand faith says “God provided for Abraham.” Firsthand faith says “you are my provider” and asks him to be that today.
  5. Ask him, not about himNext time you have a question about God, ask God first. Then check what you hear against Scripture and people you trust.
  6. Let Martha sit downThe work is not bad. But “only one thing is needed,” and it was sitting at his feet (Luke 10:41-42).

The boy Samuel lived in the house of God and “did not yet know the Lord.” God called him three times before he recognized the voice. That is normal.

  1. Expect to learn his voiceSamuel mistook God for Eli. You will mix things up too. Sheep learn a voice by being around it (John 10:4).
  2. Start with a GospelRead Mark or John slowly, a little a day, asking: what is Jesus like? You are getting to know a Person.
  3. Talk to him like he’s thereBecause he is. Short, honest, all day. “Help.” “Thank you.” “What do you think?”
  4. Find an EliEli didn’t answer for God. He taught Samuel how to answer God himself. Look for people who point you to God, not to themselves.
  5. Don’t trade it inThe pressure will come to swap the relationship for a set of rules to keep. Church, habits and teaching are good. They are not a substitute for him.
8From Sunday to every day

Four moments that turn a meeting into a walk.

Moses gave Israel a plan for keeping God’s words that had nothing to do with a building: at home, on the road, lying down, getting up (Deuteronomy 6:7). Tap a quarter of the day.

Everyday

Getting up: hand him the day before anyone else gets it.

Before the phone, before the news, before other people’s voices. David laid out his requests in the morning and then watched to see what God would do.

Try thisTwo minutes, still in bed: “Good morning. What’s on your heart today?” Then be quiet long enough to notice what comes.

On the road: the commute, the job, the in-between.

Most of life happens between appointments. Walking with God means letting him into the ordinary work, not only the spiritual part.

Try thisPick a trigger you meet every day, like a red light or a door you walk through, and let it remind you to say one honest sentence to him.

At home: where the real you lives.

Home is where faith either becomes real or stays a costume. It shows in how you treat the people who see you at your worst, and in the decisions nobody else watches.

Try thisBefore a decision today, however small, ask him first. Then notice whether peace settles or not.

Lying down: end the day with him, not with a scroll.

Go back over the day together. Where was he? What did you miss? What do you want to hand him before you sleep?

Try thisAsk two questions in the dark: “Where did I see you today?” and “Where did I go it alone?” No guilt. Just honesty.
9For leaders

The best go-between works himself out of the job.

People will always ask someone to go up the mountain for them. Good leaders go, and then take people up with them.

Moses

He wanted everyone to hear God for themselves, not to depend on him forever.

Eli

A flawed priest who got one thing exactly right. He didn’t pass on a message. He taught a boy how to answer God himself: “Say, Speak, Lord.”

John the Baptist

When people started following Jesus instead of him, he called it joy.

The new covenant

The goal of every teacher is a day when people no longer need to be told to know the Lord.

None of this means you don’t need teachers. God gave the church leaders “to equip his people” (Ephesians 4:11-12). Equip them to do what? To know him and hear him for themselves. John tells his readers that the anointing they received “teaches you about all things” (1 John 2:27). A healthy church sends you to God, not to itself.

10Status, not performance

A hired hand earns his place. A son already has one.

The younger son rehearsed a speech: “Make me like one of your hired servants.” His father never let him finish it.

This is how the older brother kept score: “All these years I’ve been slaving for you.” Salvation does not work this way, and neither does closeness.

What the father gave instead

The best robeHonor he didn’t earn
A ringAuthority in the family
SandalsThe mark of a son, not a servant

Good works are real and they matter. But Paul puts them after grace, not before it: we are saved by grace, through faith, for good works. They flow out of being his. They do not buy the right to come near.

A son still says sorry. Look at Luke 15:21: he gets out “I have sinned” and “I am no longer worthy,” and the father cuts him off before “make me a hired servant.” The confession stays. The bargain goes. Saying sorry to God isn’t paying for a way back in. It is how a son and a father talk again after something broke between them. David put it this way: “Against you, you only, have I sinned” (Psalm 51:4).

And Jesus’ own word for his disciples was not servants. It was friends.

The way is open

Go up the mountain yourself.

Israel stood at a distance from a mountain of fire. Hebrews says you have come to a different mountain, and the curtain is gone. What Moses had in the tent is offered to everyone now: face to face, as one speaks to a friend.
