Before you proceed

Every claim here wears a badge.

Volume II mapped wiring; a fair critique of tools like it is that scripture and speculation blur together. So Volume III has one rule: nothing goes unlabeled, and if a claim has no verse, it didn’t make it in. Three badges tell you exactly what kind of ground you are standing on:

Text

The Bible says this in so many words. The verse is quoted beside the claim — read it yourself.

Mirror

The parallel is drawn by Scripture itself — same words, same structure, deliberately set side by side. The claim is that the reflection exists.

Map

A teacher’s inference or an old tradition. Possibly wise, useful — but built by people. Hold it loosely; hold the text tightly.

I · The Hall of Mirrors

Twelve originals. Twelve forgeries.

A forger only copies what is precious. Walk the hall and one pattern repeats: the counterfeit is never an invention, always an inversion — the same image with the giving turned to taking. Tap any pair to see both verses side by side.

II · Galatians 5

One fruit. Many works.

Paul sets two lists four verses apart, and the grammar itself preaches: the works of the flesh are plural — a heap of disconnected deeds. The fruit of the Spirit is — singular, one organism with nine flavours. You can commit one work at a time; you cannot grow one-ninth of a fruit.

Verses 19–21

“the works of the flesh are…”

ἔργα erga — plural. Manufactured, effortful, countable. Seventeen items that don’t need each other and often can’t stand each other.

Verses 22–23

“the fruit of the Spirit is…”

καρπός karpos — singular. Grown, not built. Nine facets of one life, which is why nobody gets to specialise in three and skip the rest.

Tap a facet of the fruit

The seventeen shards

Every work of the flesh is a fruit with its Grower removed — the same appetite, torn loose from the Vine. Tap a shard to see which facet it forges.

The hidden list inside 1 Corinthians 13

Ask Paul to define love and something strange happens: the definition turns out to be the rest of the fruit — several of the very same Greek roots, verb-formed — with the works of the flesh appearing only to be negated. Love isn’t the first item on the list. It’s the whole fruit, seen ripe.

The payoff: fruit is not produced. It is borne.

Works are done; fruit is grown. The one instruction is not “try harder at all nine” but “stay attached.”

“Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me. … for without me ye can do nothing.”John 15:4–5 · KJV

III · Genesis 3 · Luke 4 · 1 John 2

There are only three doors.

Eden, the wilderness, and John’s letter describe temptation three times, centuries apart — and it is the same three doors every time. The enemy has no fourth idea. Eve faced them full, in a garden, and fell; Jesus faced them starving, in a desert, and stood. Open each door to see all three scenes aligned.

IV · Revelation 13 & 16

The borrowed trinity.

He cannot be God, so he plays God in triplicate. Revelation shows a dragon, a beast, and a false prophet running a point-for-point parody of Father, Son, and Spirit — borrowed authority, a counterfeit resurrection, fire called down on cue. And Ephesians names the everyday version every believer actually fights: the world, the flesh, and the devil, in one sentence.

V · The Whole Board

Now watch it all connect.

Everything in this atlas on one board — originals on the gold side, forgeries on the ash side, every line a verse. Then the real test: press Text only and watch how much of the web survives on the strictest evidence alone. The pattern doesn’t thin out. That is the point.

Gold lines are echoes inside the truth (shared Greek roots, quoted structures). Dashed red lines cross the seam — each one a forgery of the node it points at. Grey lines are membership. Tap any node, or use the list below.

VI · The Simplicity

It was never complicated.

That is the whole atlas. The complexity is real — seventeen shards, twelve forgeries, three doors — but it is the complexity of shattering, not of design. The design is one piece: a Vine, and branches that stay on it. The enemy’s library has one book, and it is a photocopy, printed backwards.

Sources & honesty

All quotations are the King James Version (public domain). Greek words follow the standard printed text with Strong’s numbers for lookup; where manuscripts differ the entry says so (see adultery and murders in the works list). The badge system is the contract of this volume: Text is Scripture in so many words, Mirror is a parallel the text itself draws, and Map is human inference — including some beloved traditions (the war-horse etymology of πραΰτης, Isaiah 14 read as Satan’s biography, the alignment of the three temptations). Where a claim is a map, hold it loosely — and hold the territory tightly.
