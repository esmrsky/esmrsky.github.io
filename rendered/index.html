The gift is the bottom layer.Everything above it decides how it comes out.

Most talk about spiritual gifts studies one thin layer of a person. Taylor Welch argues that three more sit on top of it: how free you are, how you were made and raised, and what you do first, before you think. The same gift runs through all of them and comes out different in every person.

One teacher’s model, laid out, then checked against Scripture and the research. Taylor Welch, The Deep End.

This page does not score you or give you a type. Given reads the gifts themselves from Scripture. This page reads the person they run through.

01Why the layers matter

A gift does not check how you are doing.

Welch starts with a verse most people quote for comfort: God does not take his gifts back. He draws the uncomfortable side out of it. If the gift stays, it keeps working, whatever state the person carrying it is in.

The first rock

He strikes it because God said to.

Water comes.
same gift
The second rock, years later

Told to speak to it, he strikes it in anger.

Water comes anyway. And Moses loses the promised land.
Episode

The gift doesn’t fact-check the person’s health. It just fires.

So a working gift proves nothing about the person using it, and a fall does not prove the gift was fake. Welch’s phrase for a gifted leader who collapses: the container could not handle the gift. Which is why he refuses to start with the gift. He starts with the container.

EpisodeScripture

The fruit tattletales on the seed.

The ground does not ask what you intended to grow. What comes up tells you what was planted. Jesus said the same about people.

CorrectedHe says the second rock is in Numbers 28.

It is Numbers 20:8–12. Exodus 17:6 is the first.

Context“God’s gifts and his call are irrevocable.”

Paul says it about Israel’s calling (Romans 11:28–29). Applying it to each believer’s gifts is a common extension, and Welch’s point still stands on the Moses story alone.

V5Freedom: the top layer

Free or bound. The lens on everything below.

Before he asks which gift you have, Welch asks how free you are. The same gift in a free person and in a bound person can look like two different gifts.

Free

The gift is used. Nothing is needed from it.

No self-protection, no need for approval, no status game. The person does what God asks with it and goes home.

Bound

The gift is a way to feel safe, special or chosen.

“The person who is bound, they must use their gift to make themselves feel better about their gift and who they are.” The gift still works. It is just working for them now.

Episode: the tipping point

The moment you’re more interested in being seen in your gifts than being obedient with your gifts, you’ve tilted into unhealth.

The past

How you carry what already happened. Rumination: what did I do, could I have stopped it.

The future

What you expect is coming for you.

He calls this one “complicated” and says no more in this episode.

Permission

Whether you let yourself succeed. Worthiness, and the quiet ways people sabotage themselves.

Armor

How much you protect yourself from being known. Are you comfortable with who you are?

The four places Welch looks to measure freedom. On Given they appear as “Welch’s grid” beside each gift. Armor is personal for him: “My wife learned that you perform so that you were safe, and I learned that you perform so you were chosen.” Same armor, two reasons.

Both ends are the same wound.

A bound gift rarely fails in only one direction. It swings. Welch names both ends for three gifts; the free form is the still point between them.

Quits earlyFreeControls
Free

Builds what God asks, stays as long as God says, then lets others hold it.

Vision falls out of this person. People want to work beside them. Welch calls it the apostolic, container-building gift: God speaks, and someone builds the structure that can carry what he said.

Episode “Both of these are the same unhealth.” Welch, of the two ends of his own story.
Bound: quits early

Leaves while it is going well, before it can be lost.

Welch’s own story. He built something, was proud of it, and lost it. His past became “a prison”: what did I do, could I have changed it. In the next thing, when it was doing well, he quit before he could lose it again.

Ask: am I starting the next thing because God asked, or to escape the last one before I lose it?

Bound: controls

Stays, and holds everyone so tightly nothing can be lost.

He saw the quitting and tried to fix it by willpower. He stayed, and found himself “controlling everything and everyone,” so there would be nothing left to regret. The other shape of the same gift: the founder who won’t leave after God says go. “Nothing’s more scary than an apostle who will not leave.”

Free

Says the hard thing, in love, and stays in the room.

Hears what God is saying about now and says it, including what costs them. Welch has “mass respect” for this gift. It cuts deep and it risks rejection every time.

Bound: runs

Delivers the word, then disappears.

“Prophet always runs if they’re unhealthy.” Elijah ran from Jezebel a chapter after fire fell from heaven. Jonah ran the other way before he said anything at all.

Bound: flatters

Swings to good news for everyone, to be accepted.

The other end of the runner: a prophet who only says what lifts people, for status and reverence. Welch: you are “now being used by your gift to make sure that you are safe and significant.”

Checked His test, “show me all the times you’ve hidden,” is a provocation, not a rule. Scripture tests a prophet by the words, not by a record of hiding (1 Thessalonians 5:21).
Free

Gives freely, cheerfully, and mostly in secret.

Releases money and resources without keeping score or needing it back. “There’s power in secrecy when it comes to giving.” His wife is his model: she once carried a stack of ten-dollar gift cards to hand to whoever needed one.

Bound: gives to get

Giving as a purchase: of access, of return, of being known as generous.

Tracking how much you gave against others. Needing people to know. “The more I give, the more I get” turned from a promise into a transaction.

Bound: hoards

The first time the return doesn’t come, the hand closes.

Give-to-get swings to scarcity, and scarcity hoards. Welch: “If he can wound a giving person enough, they’ll begin to hoard.”

Episode

You typically do have the highest concentration of wounds around where you’re most gifted.

His reason: the enemy cannot make a gift, so he cannot use one unless he takes yours. “One of the best ways to commandeer a gift is just wound them repeatedly in their area of gifting.” He adds a brake on his own idea: “I don’t want to give the devil credit for everything. Sometimes it’s just us.”

Our readingScripture

Scripture shows the pattern without making it a law. Peter, the one who would steady the others, is the one Satan asks to sift, and Jesus answers with prayer, not a warning to hide the gift.

TrainingHow are you doing it?

Skill. A real gift used clumsily needs practice and feedback: unclear explanations, no story, too long to land.

HealingWhy are you doing it?

Motive. No skill fixes it. Welch: you usually have to look at the past to find what needs healing.

V2–4Seed, house, story

Footage, grade, titles.

Under freedom, Welch puts how a person was made. He explains it with a picture from video editing: the raw footage, the color grade laid over it, and the titles that tell the viewer what they are watching. A gift can only come out in the ways these three allow.

Seed the footage

What you were born with.

Nature, temperament, the bloodline.

The source image. Welch includes inherited leanings here, good and bad, and reads “bloodline curses” as a permission slip written into the family line until someone removes it.

House the grade

How you were raised.

Nurture: the dials turned on the footage.

Early experience unlocks some things in a personality and mutes others. His two oldest share footage and were graded differently: one an “in-your-face truth teller” like him, one gentler, like their mom.

Story the titles

What gets told about you.

The Hebrew toledot: “the account of.”

The running record: this house did this, this person did that. Genesis is organized by these accounts, and every person carries one, told by others and by themselves.

Episode: his analogy, made to move

Same dial. Different response time.

Early on, Welch says, you move the dial and see it at once. Later “it’ll take seven days, or six years.” Pick an age, then turn a dial.

Age 2: the picture answers almost as you turn.
Episode

Past the window, change is a re-render.

After childhood, Welch says, changing a person means re-rendering the whole sequence, and that “typically looks like pain, suffering, loss, affliction, pressure.” He even says betrayal can be the price of the very thing you begged God for.

Hard to render. One of his questions: do you often say “this is how I’ve always been”? If so, he says, “you will have a lot of hidden gifts that you just don’t express.” This is the closest the episode comes to its title’s “dormant gifts.”

Our readingScripture

Scripture agrees that suffering can produce character. It does not make pain the only tool, or God its author. The New Testament’s main word for being changed is renewal: of the mind, by looking at the Lord, day by day.

Pain is where many people finally hold still long enough to be changed. The changing is still his kindness.

Overstated“After about 8 or 9, personality is somewhat static, very cemented.”

Personality keeps changing through adulthood. A review of long-running studies found the largest average changes between ages 20 and 40 (Roberts, Walton and Viechtbauer, 2006). Early years matter a great deal; they do not close the window.

Roughly rightThe front of the brain is still maturing in the teens and into the mid-20s.

That matches the standard summary (NIMH, “The Teen Brain”). His exact percentages were banter, not data.

Not established“Bloodline curses” as a permission slip written into the DNA.

Epigenetic marks can pass down generations in plants and some animals; in humans that kind of inheritance is not established (Heard and Martienssen, 2014). Scripture holds two things together: consequences reach children (Exodus 20:5–6), and guilt does not pass to them (Ezekiel 18:20).

His synthesis“In the Old Testament there’s three levels of identity programming.”

The words are biblical and toledot is real; the three-level scheme is his. The verse he gives for “house,” “As for me and my household, we will serve the Lord” (Joshua 24:15), is about choosing, not upbringing.

V1Domains: the instinct layer

What you do first, before you think.

The layer just above the gift is the domain: not what you can do, but your first move. Welch names four. They hardly change after about twenty, he says. The gifts riding on them can move.

Strategic

Steps back and maps it.

“Canvas first.” Open a notebook, find the broken process, look at the structure.

Often: apostle, teacher

Execution

Does something, now.

Meets the need, rallies people, gets it moving before the meeting ends.

Often: serving, giving, leading

Relating

Calls the person.

Connects first. The problem is a person before it is a problem.

Often: shepherd, mercy

Influencing

Changes how people see it.

“In changing people you will change the problem.”

Often: prophet, evangelist, exhortation
Confirmed“Very similar to the Gallup profiles.”

More than similar: his four names match the four domains of Gallup’s CliftonStrengths, which are Strategic Thinking, Executing, Relationship Building and Influencing. What he adds is placing the spiritual gifts under them.

EpisodeAn instinct, not an emotion.

When a co-host said his first move would be to call and apologize because he felt bad for wasting Welch’s time, Welch didn’t read it as fear of man. That is a relating instinct.

Hunt the gift by what breaks your heart.

“You can hunt down the gift based on what makes you so sad and angry.” His wife is undone by needs she cannot meet. He is undone by systems that keep producing desperate people. Same scene, two gifts.

Domains stay. The order of gifts moves.

Healing changes what a domain does to you, Welch says, not which domain you have. What changes with your season is which gifts are on top. More on that next.

EpisodeScripture

With some gifts, the gift is you.

Romans 12 lists things people do. Ephesians 4 lists people Christ gives to the church. Welch draws the line there: a teacher brings a cluster of things with them, but “you’re the gift.” He admits he is “mixing offices with gifts” in one list of ten, because he thinks they connect.

And before any of it, Paul asks for sober judgment, not a high opinion.

FXPractice

Clean the motive in the moment.

Welch’s answer to mixed motives is not a long self-examination. It is one picture, held for a few seconds, right when the chance to use your gift shows up.

Step 1 of 5

Picture your worst fear coming true.

Not a vague one. The specific thing you would least want to lose.

Now make it worse.

Everyone you love watches you suffer, lose everything and be humiliated, and there is nothing they can do.

And you choose it anyway, for someone else.

Would that be hard? Would it be terrifying?

Jesus did that. And he chose it.

Then ask: Christ, and him crucified. How does he want me to use this?

Do it again ten minutes later, when the next chance comes. “The motives will begin to fall off of you.”

Then ask God to describe you

“God, if you were describing me to your best friend, how would you describe me? I’m ready. I’m going to write it down.”

Welch’s closing practice. He tells how, after a fight on a family trip, he asked God how to see his wife, and heard that he had been using his teaching and leadership gift on her. “You are not in trouble, but you are slowing me down.”

EpisodeScripture

“Don’t lean on the profile. Lean on what God thinks about you.” And test what you hear, like anything else. Conviction from the Spirit, he says, “never produces guilt. It produces repentance.”

QCQuality control

Every strong claim, checked.

A long, unscripted conversation makes big claims in passing. Here are the ones that carry weight, with what holds and what doesn’t.

CorrectedMoses strikes the rock a second time in “Numbers 28.”

Numbers 20:8–12.

CorrectedThe manifestation gifts are in “1 Corinthians 9.”

A co-host’s guess, corrected on air: 1 Corinthians 12.

Corrected“Galatians 3 or Galatians 6” repeats Romans 12:3.

Galatians 6:3: “If anyone thinks they are something when they are not, they deceive themselves.”

ContextGifts are irrevocable (Romans 11:29).

The verse is about Israel’s calling. Extending it to every believer’s gifts is common; the Moses story makes his point without it.

His synthesisSeed, house and story are “three levels of identity programming” in the Old Testament.

The words are biblical; the scheme is his. Joshua 24:15 is about choosing whom to serve, not about upbringing.

ConfirmedHis four domains are “very similar to Gallup.”

They are Gallup CliftonStrengths’ four domains under shorter names.

OverstatedPersonality is “cemented” by about age 8.

It keeps changing through adulthood, most of all between 20 and 40 (Roberts, Walton and Viechtbauer, 2006).

Roughly rightThe frontal lobe is still maturing in the teens and mid-20s.

The standard summary (NIMH). The percentages traded on air were jokes.

Not establishedBloodline curses are written into DNA.

Inherited epigenetic marks across generations are not established in humans (Heard and Martienssen, 2014).

Overstated“The brain doesn’t reorganize around information. It reorganizes around survival.”

People learn from ordinary information all the time. The idea he leans on, that surprise against what you expected drives learning, is real: it is called prediction error (Schultz, Dayan and Montague, 1997).

His observationWounds concentrate where you are most gifted.

Plausible and pastorally useful. Not testable as stated, and he limits it himself: “Sometimes it’s just us.”

His reading“Every single crisis in the history of the world is because a gift was used outside of the jurisdiction of God’s will.”

Sweeping and unprovable as stated. The useful core is narrower: a gift works whether or not it is surrendered, as Babel shows (Genesis 11).

His readingJesus carried every gift, because he was constantly renewed.

Speculative, but it sits close to John 3:34: God “gives the Spirit without limit.”

His estimate“90%” of what people call church hurt is a pastor who didn’t notice them.

An estimate from experience. No data was given.
