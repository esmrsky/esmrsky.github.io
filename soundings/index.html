Descend

In one sense it's simple. Maturity is walking in love.

Everything below is scaffolding for that sentence. Paul lists the things churches actually measure — eloquence, prophecy, knowledge, mountain-moving faith, giving everything away, even martyrdom — and says that without love they add up to nothing. Not less. Nothing.

1 Cor 13 — without love, nothing Gal 5:6 — faith working through love John 13:35 — by this they'll know 1 John 4:8 — whoever doesn't love doesn't know God REVEAL defined growth as increasing love for God and people
So why the rest of the page? Because "just love people" is true and almost useless as instruction. Everyone already agrees. What nobody can see is what's in the way — and that's different at every depth. The infant can't love yet because nothing is furnished. The child can't because the sentences still run back to himself. The young adult loves hard and burns out because he's confused love with usefulness. The map doesn't replace the measure. It tells you where the obstacle is.

Ezekiel is led into a river and measured four times: ankles, knees, waist, then water he can't cross. Maturity runs the same direction — not more footing, less.

Between waist-deep and over your head

The Wall is where most people stop.

Hagberg and Guelich found it standing between the productive life and everything after it — where your will meets God's, face to face. Almost nobody gets warned.

approach · stall · retreat · repeat

What it feels like

  • What used to produce fruit stops working
  • Certainty fails — not belief, certainty
  • Prayer goes quiet, and reading about prayer doesn't help
  • Buried things surface, usually with a name attached
  • True words from other Christians land wrong

What it is not

  • Not backsliding — most people keep leading throughout
  • Not punishment. "Find the hidden sin" is the most damaging response
  • Not clinical depression, though they often run together. If sleep, appetite or your will to keep going are affected, see a doctor as well
  • Not optional, if the far side is the goal
2 exits

Backwards into busier, more visible productivity — where the machinery still works and nobody asks. Or through: solitude, grief, honest company, real help with old wounds. Hagberg is blunt that you can't go through the Wall on sixty-hour weeks. Most take the first exit and call it faithfulness.

≈1 in 4

The share of people in the churches REVEAL surveyed who called themselves stalled or dissatisfied — concentrated among the most committed. That's what a building full of people at the Wall looks like from outside.

Sharpest disagreement: doubt

  • Putman: questioning is usually childishness
  • Fowler & Peck: it's the engine of the most important transition — inherited faith to owned faith
  • Hagberg: it is the Wall
  • Where you land decides whether a doubting 30-year-old is a problem or in labour

Second: what maturity even is

  • Putman's endpoint is reproduction — disciples who make disciples
  • Teresa, Hagberg and Rohr's endpoint is union and self-forgetting
  • By their measure a prolific disciple-maker who never hit the Wall is still stage three
  • Held together they check each other: fruit without depth burns out; depth without fruit curdles

Third: four maps have no Wall at all

  • 1 John names three groups and simply leaves the gap where the Wall goes
  • Putman, REVEAL and Spader are the three most-used modern discipleship models — and none has a category for a faith that works less well as it matures
  • All three are excellent at moving people from dry ground to waist-deep. That is what they were built for
  • The cost: a church running them reads the Wall as regression, and prescribes more of waist-deep — which is the one thing that reliably doesn't work

The pattern nobody tells you

Peck noticed we're mostly threatened by the people one stage above us. Which means the friction in your church is rarely about music, translations or the building fund — it's people at different depths finding each other intolerable and reaching for the nearest available issue. It also means the best person to help someone is usually not the most mature one available. It's someone one depth ahead, close enough to remember the water at that height.

Read before using on a human being

Seven honest limits.

01 / measurementThe best-known study is inconclusive

Sociologist Bradley Wright found REVEAL's design and analysis weak and heavily over-interpreted. The famous finding — church activity doesn't drive growth — rings true to most pastors. That isn't the same as established.

02 / self-reportYou are the worst judge of this

Knee-deep reliably rates itself higher, because fluency feels like depth from inside. Treat any reading you produce alone as a hypothesis. The people who live with you hold the evidence.

03 / definitionsFowler isn't measuring Christian faith

He defines faith as universal meaning-making — structure, not content — so an atheist progresses through the same stages. Useful on how someone holds beliefs; useless on whether they're true.

04 / shapeNone of it is linear

You move backwards under grief, illness, betrayal and exhaustion. You can be over your head in suffering and knee-deep in conflict the same month. Hagberg's "home stage" beats any language of rank.

05 / sampleBuilt on a narrow slice of humanity

Mostly Western, literate, church-attending adults in the late twentieth century; the classical model came out of enclosed religious orders. Cultural bias in Fowler is a standing criticism.

06 / attributionThe Paul descent assumes disputed authorship

His self-estimate falling — least of the apostles, then very least of all the saints, then foremost of sinners — runs across 1 Corinthians, Ephesians and 1 Timothy. Critical scholarship contests Pauline authorship of the last two, which would make the descent a portrait drawn by his students rather than a self-report. The pattern still holds as early testimony about him; it is weaker as autobiography.

07 / the mapEzekiel's vision isn't about you

It's about a river that heals a dead sea. Using it as a maturity chart is an application, not an exegesis. If the framework ever gets more interesting than the person, you've lost the thread.
