The premise

The cost of producing code is collapsing. The value of judging it is exploding. For thirty years, learning to build meant memorizing how to type the right incantations. AI ended that. What's left — and what this site teaches — is the part that was always the real skill: understanding how systems fit together, saying precisely what you want, and recognizing whether what you got is any good.

That means some classic advice is now wrong. You don't need to grind syntax. You don't need 800 LeetCode problems. You do need to read code, hold the Git model in your head, think in systems, and develop taste — because you're not the typist anymore. You're the editor, the reviewer, and the one who decides what gets built.

Everything here is deliberately opinionated. Where reasonable people disagree, we picked a side and said why. Strong opinions you can argue with beat hedges you can't use.

Where to start

🌱 Never built anything?

Read the Glossary top to bottom — just the plain-English parts, skip every "go deeper." Then look at the Timeline to see why now is a strangely lucky moment to start.

🔧 Built a bit, drowning in terms?

Go straight to the Skills Map and ruthlessly cut what you're studying down to the green column. Use the Glossary's Git diagrams to finally make branches click.

🧭 Deciding what to bet your time on?

Start with the AI Landscape — especially the weak-and-slow-moving spots, because that's where humans stay valuable — then sanity-check your plan against the Skills Map.
