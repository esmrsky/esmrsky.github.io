Tuned.

You notice what you're tuned to. What tunes you is your state. Your state is set by your body, what you believe, and who you're around. One state opens everything else: noticing, taste, play, desire, connection. That state is safety. And the deepest safety isn't the absence of threat. It's trust. Peace is not a frequency. It's a state. And it's contagious.

Two pendulums, one shared beam. Watching them drift.

This page separates what has been measured from what we are inferring from what scripture says. You will see the three side by side the whole way down.

The spine

Two loops, running in opposite directions. Almost everything below is downstream of which one is running: why a song lands, why a room feels heavy, why you can't have fun, why a verse is dead one day and alive the next.

The open loop

Runs on safety.

Trust

You are safe with someone who is for you. Not a feeling yet. A settled fact.

1 of 7

The closed loop

Runs on threat.

Fear

The body reads threat. It is often right, which is why this loop exists at all.

1 of 5

Same person, same world, same Bible verses. Which loop you are in determines what you can see, what you want, and who you can be with.

The loops are not moral categories. Everyone is in the closed loop sometimes. The body puts you there when it reads threat, and it is often right.

01

Resonance

In physics, resonance is what happens when energy arrives at the rate something already wants to vibrate at, so small pushes add up. Between people, resonance is a metaphor, but a good one. Nervous systems do fall into sync. Through the eyes and ears, though, not through the air as vibration.

What resonance actually isA

Every object that can oscillate has a natural frequency, set by its mass and its stiffness. When energy arrives at that same rate, each push lands in time with the motion already there, so the pushes add instead of cancelling. A swing pushed at the right moment. A tuning fork that hums when its twin is struck across the room. A wine glass shattered by a sung note, which is real but demanding: the singer has to find that particular glass's resonant peak and hold it at roughly 105 decibels.

Two conditions, both required. The frequencies have to match, and something physical has to carry the energy between them.

Entrainment: things drift into syncA

In February 1665, bedridden and watching two of his pendulum clocks, Christiaan Huygens noticed what he called an odd kind of sympathy. Left alone, the two clocks settled into a fixed relationship and held it. He first suspected air currents, then worked out that the coupling ran through the beam both clocks hung from. He wrote it up to the Royal Society that same month.

The detail usually dropped from the retelling is the one worth keeping. They did not end up swinging together. They ended up swinging exactly opposite, and that is why the state is stable: in opposition the two pendulums pull on the beam in equal and contrary directions, the beam stops moving, and no more energy leaks away. In step, they shake the beam hard and lose energy until they stop being in step. The pendulums at the top of this page are running that physics, not a scripted animation.

Weakly coupled oscillators tending to synchronize is a general result, formalised later in the Kuramoto model, and it turns up in fireflies, cardiac pacemaker cells and neurons. This is the physics that gives the metaphor its footing.

Does it apply to people?B
Measured

Ruth Feldman's work on parent and infant pairs finds heart rhythms coupling during episodes of interactive synchrony, when gaze, voice and affect are coordinated together. Greg Stephens, Lauren Silbert and Uri Hasson (2010) showed a listener's brain activity tracking a speaker's with a lag, and in some regions running slightly ahead of it, with tighter coupling predicting better comprehension. Musicians playing together sync physiologically.

Inferred

"We fell into the same rhythm" is more than a figure of speech. But the medium is perception and mimicry. The coupling runs through the senses, not through a field.

Scripture

"Rejoice with those who rejoice, weep with those who weep." Romans 12:15. The instruction assumes emotional coupling is possible, and asks you to do it on purpose.

Where it breaksC

Emotions are not oscillations. There is no peace frequency in the nervous system that another person's body could match.

The Word sustaining creation is a strong claim on its own and does not need physics. "In him all things hold together" (Colossians 1:17); "he upholds the universe by the word of his power" (Hebrews 1:3). Matter is held together by electromagnetic and nuclear forces, and if atoms stopped vibrating nothing would explode. You would have something very cold and very still. Borrowing physics the claim does not have makes the theology look weaker than it is.

02

Tuning

Mood is not just what you think. It is what you think, filtered through what your body is doing, filtered through who you are around. And mood picks what you notice, which reinforces the mood. That loop is the honest explanation for what resonates with you this week.

What you're tuned to

Sixty shapes. Pick one and watch the field reorganise itself.

Nothing is selected. Sixty shapes, no particular reason to see any of them.

The top-down halfA

Appraisal theory: feelings come from how you interpret a situation, and interpretation is shaped by what you believe about yourself and the world. Identity acts as a filter on what even registers. Two people get the same email. One reads hostility, one reads haste.

The bottom-up half, which is the one people skipA

Sleep, blood sugar, illness, hormones, light, caffeine, whether you moved today. These set the baseline your thoughts get coloured by. People who treat mood as purely a belief problem end up trying to think their way out of a body problem.

Scripture

Elijah under the broom tree asks to die. An angel wakes him, feeds him and lets him sleep. Then wakes him, feeds him and lets him sleep again. Only after that does he walk to Horeb and get a word. 1 Kings 19:4-9. The order matters.

The loop closes: mood picks attentionA

Mood-congruent processing: you preferentially notice and remember things that match the state you are already in. Combine that with the frequency illusion, where something that has just become salient starts appearing everywhere, and you get the full "everything connects this week" experience.

Inferred

The feeling of confirmation is produced by the same machinery whether the thing is true or not. So it cannot be the test of whether something is from God. Scripture's tests are external for exactly this reason: agreement with what is written, fruit, and counsel. 1 John 4:1, 1 Thessalonians 5:21, Proverbs 11:14.

The state that changes everythingB

Play, exploration, curiosity and social openness are gated by felt safety. This is the single most important claim on the page. Safety is not one mood among many. It is the precondition for the good ones.

Measured

Jaak Panksepp found that young rats stop playing almost entirely when they smell cat odour, and stay suppressed for days afterwards. Later work found the effect is sex-dependent, so it is not quite as simple as it first looked. In John Bowlby and Mary Ainsworth's attachment work, children explore when a caregiver is present and stop when they are not. Under threat the body narrows attention, drops long-term thinking, and pulls back from other people.

Scripture

"In peace I will both lie down and sleep; for you alone, O Lord, make me dwell in safety." Psalm 4:8
"You keep him in perfect peace whose mind is stayed on you, because he trusts in you." Isaiah 26:3
"In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and in trust shall be your strength." Isaiah 30:15

Where it breaksC

Polyvagal theory is the popular framework for all of this. Its general claim, that autonomic state gates your capacity for social engagement, is well supported. Its specific anatomical and evolutionary story is not: Paul Grossman and Edwin Taylor have pressed that case for years and a recent multi-author paper argues the specific version is untenable, which Stephen Porges disputes. Use the general claim. Do not lean on the circuitry.

The other failure mode is closer to home. "Manage your state" can quietly become a new self-audit, which is the closed loop wearing a wellness jacket. The Bible's version is "casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you" (1 Peter 5:7), not keeping your vibe clean.

03

The Word

The popular teaching says logos is the written, general word and rhema is the specific, spoken word to you. The Greek does not support that split. The experience it is trying to name is real, though, and the Bible has better language for it. The difference is never in the text. It is in the hearer and the Spirit.

What the Greek actually doesA

Both words come from legō, to speak. Logos appears about 330 times in the New Testament, rhema about 70. There is a mild difference in flavour: rhema leans towards the individual utterance, logos towards the message as a whole. Not written against spoken. Not dead against alive.

  • Hebrews 4:12, "the word of God is living and active," uses logos.
  • John 1 uses logos for the living person of Christ.
  • Rhema in Luke often just means "thing". Luke 2:15, let us go and see this thing that has happened. Luke 1:37, no rhema will be impossible, which the ESV renders simply as "nothing".
  • John 12:48 uses both in one sentence for the same referent: the rhemata a person refuses, and the logos that will judge them.
  • John 6:63 and 6:68 both use rhema, for the same words of Jesus that verse 68 calls the words of eternal life.
  • In the Septuagint both translate the single Hebrew word dabar.
What the Bible says about the differenceS
Scripture

Same seed, different soil. In the parable of the sower the word is identical in all four cases. What varies is reception. Matthew 13:1-23

Letter and Spirit. "For the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life." 2 Corinthians 3:6. This is the verse people are reaching for when they say rhema.

Opened scriptures, burning hearts. "Did not our hearts burn within us while he talked to us on the road, while he opened to us the Scriptures?" Luke 24:32. The same texts they had read all their lives. Christ opened them.

Open my eyes. "Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of your law." Psalm 119:18, and "having the eyes of your hearts enlightened" (Ephesians 1:18). The prayer is for sight, not for new text.

The better vocabularyC

Illumination is the classic term. Quickening is the older one. Simplest of all: the word as information against the word as address. Third person against second person.

"I love you" printed on a card in a shop, against hearing it from someone who means you. A pamphlet about an illness, against the same pamphlet the day after the diagnosis. The words did not change. You became the person they were addressed to.

Kierkegaard, in For Self-Examination, says to read scripture the way a lover reads a letter from the beloved, applying every line to yourself rather than examining it. Luther's version is pro me, for me.

Why the framing mattersC

If "God gave me a rhema" is a separate category of word, it can quietly become a trump card over the written text, and people get hurt by that. If the living word is the same word illuminated, it can never contradict the page it came off. That is a safety rail, not a technicality.

The connection to stateC

Illumination and safety are linked. A verse rarely comes alive while you are in the closed loop, because fear narrows attention to the threat. "Be still, and know that I am God" (Psalm 46:10) puts stillness before knowing.

This is not a technique for making verses land. It is a description of why they usually land in rest.

04

Presence

People do carry something, and it lands in the first few seconds. The channels are all identifiable: face, posture, voice, pace, even scent. Places carry something too, but it is ambient information plus human intent plus your own history. The awareness lives in the reader, not in the room.

Thin slicesA

Nalini Ambady and Robert Rosenthal showed that strangers watching a few seconds of silent video judge warmth, competence and some personality traits well above chance. This runs on a channel you cannot introspect, which is why the output arrives as a feeling in the air rather than as a list of cues.

How stress and peace actually travelB
Measured

Nonverbal leakage: micro-expressions, posture, gaze, movement speed. Voice: a stressed voice runs higher, tighter and jitterier, and listeners pick it up in fractions of a second. This is the one place where vibrating through the air is literally true, because voice is vibration through air.

Elaine Hatfield, John Cacioppo and Richard Rapson's emotional contagion model has us mimicking faces and postures unconsciously, with the mimicry feeding back into our own feeling. The mimicry is solid. The feedback step took a hit when a seventeen-lab replication of the pen-in-mouth facial feedback study came back null, so hold that half loosely. On chemosignals: Jasper de Groot and colleagues, and Lilianne Mujica-Parodi's skydiver study, found that sweat produced in fear, smelled without the smeller knowing, primes fear-congruent responses. A 2017 meta-analysis of twenty-six studies supports a small to moderate real effect. The specific claim that it raises sustained vigilance is weaker: de Groot's own later work found faster threat detection but not sustained vigilance. Stress does travel through the air. As molecules.

Scripture

"For we are the aroma of Christ to God among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing, to one a fragrance from death to death, to the other a fragrance from life to life." 2 Corinthians 2:15-16. Presence as scent, registered before you speak, landing differently depending on who is smelling.

"I can feel them judging me"B

Real: you are reading a held gaze, a tightened mouth, a flat tone, a half-second delay.

Also real: the feeling of being judged shows up reliably when nobody is judging, driven by your own state. From the inside the two are indistinguishable. The feeling is evidence about you at least as much as about them.

PlacesB

Three layers, stacked.

Ambient information. Light, ceiling height, sound reflection, materials, temperature, smell, visual complexity. Decades of environmental psychology on how these shift mood. Cathedrals work on atheists.

Encoded human intent. A car's vibe was designed by people using your own face-reading machinery: headlights as eyes, grille as mouth. A city's feel is millions of decisions about scale and pace.

Your history, and the only reader you have. We are built to see intention in rustling grass, so we read mood into weather and awareness into rooms.

Scripture

Places are holy in the Bible because of who is there, or what happened there. Take off your sandals comes after the bush is burning (Exodus 3). And Jacob at Bethel: "Surely the Lord is in this place, and I did not know it." Genesis 28:16. He had slept there feeling nothing at all until the encounter.

Where it breaksC

Mirror neurons are real and oversold. They do not explain sensing someone's spirit. The heart's electromagnetic field is measurable and far too weak to affect anyone at a distance.

The feeling of being stared at from behind is the interesting one, because the honest answer is messier than either side wants. Edward Titchener dismissed it in 1898 but published no data or method. The controlled tests since have produced a contested record rather than a clean failure, including a pair of studies where a sceptic and a proponent running the same protocol got different results, which points at experimenter effects more than at a sense. What you can reliably detect is peripheral motion, sound, and a room going quiet.

The bias towards over-reading agency, Justin Barrett's hyperactive agency detection, is a plausible and widely cited hypothesis, but direct support for the hyperactive part is thin, so treat it as a story rather than a mechanism. Panpsychism is a serious philosophical position with no empirical handle, and it is not the biblical picture either. Scripture personifies creation as poetry, not as physics.

David and Saul, which is not the story people use it forS

In 1 Samuel 16 the harp refreshes Saul and the harmful spirit departs, and music therapy confirms the general effect. But the chapter opens with the Spirit coming on David and leaving Saul. The narrator's frame is who the Spirit is with, not what the harp was tuned to.

And twice later, in 18:10-11 and 19:9-10, Saul throws a spear at David while David is playing. The harp was relief, not cure, and the presence David carried did not guarantee the room went well. Paul was told to his face that his bodily presence was weak and his speech of no account (2 Corinthians 10:10).

Presence in the biblical sense is not a field you tune and project. It is fruit, and people register it mostly through how you treat them.

05

Taste

A style is other people's material passed through a filter, and the filter is the person. Nearly everything you like is borrowed. What is yours is what you keep, what you drop, what you return to, and how you combine it under constraint. That is not a lesser thing. It is the only way anyone has ever had a style.

What's borrowedB

Mere exposure. Robert Zajonc, 1968: repeated contact makes you like something more. One of the better replicated findings in the field.

Processing fluency. Things your brain parses easily feel good. Familiar structure with a twist beats pure novelty. Raymond Loewy's MAYA, most advanced yet acceptable, is the designer's aphorism for it rather than a research finding, and Daniel Berlyne's inverted-U curve of arousal and liking was the mid-century attempt to measure it, now treated as historically important rather than current consensus.

Social signalling. Pierre Bourdieu's Distinction argues that taste marks and reproduces class position. A lot of "my style" is "my people's style".

Mimetic desire. René Girard: we want what we see others wanting, and we want others to want what we want. A theory, not a measurement, and a sharp one.

On the popular claim that musical taste locks in around early adolescence: there is a real sensitive period somewhere across the teens and into the twenties, but the studies disagree about where the peak sits, and a 2021 re-analysis found little evidence of durable lock-in at all. Treat "the music of your youth" as a tendency, not a law.

What's yoursB

The selection function. Everyone gets roughly the same firehose. The difference is what you keep.

Temperament is substantially heritable, and openness, sensory sensitivity and tolerance for ambiguity all drive the filter. Making things feeds back into taste: you learn to like what you have learned to see, which is why a designer's eye sharpens and narrows over a career.

Scripture on tasteS

Genesis 2:9 has God making the trees "pleasant to the sight and good for food," in that order. Aesthetic preference is built in before utility.

In Exodus 31 the Spirit fills Bezalel specifically for craft, which is the first time in the Bible anyone is said to be filled with the Spirit at all.

And the Bible's own authors were remixing. The Psalms use Canaanite poetic forms, Psalm 29 especially. Proverbs 22:17 to 24:22 overlaps the Egyptian Instruction of Amenemope, which predates it. Paul quotes Epimenides and Aratus in Acts 17:28, Epimenides again in Titus 1:12, and Menander in 1 Corinthians 15:33.

Where it breaksC

"Everyone should be into this" is mimetic desire running outward. It is normal, and it is the root of both evangelism and the pressure that makes people perform a taste, or a faith, they do not have.

Also: a board is not a filter. Collecting everything that ever resonated and reusing it on everything produces sameness. Taste is as much what you refuse.

06

Desire and fun

Wanting and enjoying run on different systems, and they can come apart. Fun is what happens when you are safe enough to play: a little novelty, a little challenge, no audit. You cannot have fun while afraid. That is not a character flaw. The body turns play off under threat.

Wanting and liking, by state

Same person, same object of desire. Only the state changes.

Wanting
55
Liking
55

Wanting and liking are different systemsA

Kent Berridge and Terry Robinson's distinction, built up over three decades: dopamine drives wanting, meaning pursuit, craving and anticipation. Liking, the actual pleasure, runs on separate opioid and related systems.

They usually travel together. Under stress, novelty-seeking or addiction, wanting climbs while liking flattens. You chase harder and enjoy less.

What fun is made ofB

Play is one of the primary emotional systems in mammals, in Panksepp's account, and it switches off under fear. Rats stop playing when they smell a cat.

Flow, in Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's description, needs challenge slightly above skill, clear feedback, and self-consciousness dropping out. Optimal arousal says too familiar is boring and too novel is threatening, and that the sweet spot moves with your state.

Inferred

Fun equals safe novelty, plus a little challenge, plus nobody keeping score. Remove any one of the three and it stops being fun. This is why the same activity is fun with one group and stressful with another.

Desire as compass, desire as tyrantC

Desire is a signal about what you are tuned to, and it is real information. But wanting without liking is the signature of the closed loop: pursuit that never lands. The question is not "do I want this". It is "does this land, and does it still land afterwards".

Scripture

"Delight yourself in the Lord, and he will give you the desires of your heart." Psalm 37:4. Read in order: delight first, then desire is reshaped, then given.

Inferred

Augustine's ordo amoris, rightly ordered loves: nothing is wrong to want, and things go wrong when a lesser love is put in the place of a greater one. James K. A. Smith's version is that you are what you love, and what you love is formed by what you practise.

Joy and safetyS

"In your presence there is fullness of joy." Psalm 16:11

"Do not be grieved, for the joy of the Lord is your strength." Nehemiah 8:10

"There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear." 1 John 4:18

"The Lord your God is in your midst, a mighty one who will save; he will rejoice over you with gladness; he will quiet you by his love; he will exult over you with loud singing." Zephaniah 3:17

Notice the order in Zephaniah: presence, gladness, quieting, singing. Safety, then joy, then music.

Where it breaksC

Fun can become a wanting-without-liking loop of its own. The scroll, the next episode, the next purchase. The test is the same one: does it land, and does it leave you more or less able to be with people afterwards.

07

Music

Music is the clearest place where all of this meets. It is physical, it moves your heart rate and your hormones. It is tied to state, so the same song is medicine one day and noise the next. It is identity, because your genre says who your people are. And it synchronises rooms.

What music does to the bodyB

Valorie Salimpoor and colleagues found dopamine release during musical chills, in the same circuitry as food and reward, and separated the anticipation from the peak. It is a landmark study with a very small imaging sample, eight people, since extended by larger follow-ups.

Slow music lowering heart rate and cortisol is supported, though the picture is not tidy: one trial of a fifteen-day slow-music intervention found reduced cortisol and blood pressure, while a separate study found cortisol fell more with fast tempo. Take the direction, not the dose.

Singing together bonds people unusually fast. Eiluned Pearce, Jacques Launay and Robin Dunbar's ice-breaker study followed adult education classes for seven months and found singing groups grew close faster than craft or creative writing groups. Note that this study found no pain-threshold difference. The pain-threshold result people cite comes from separate Dunbar lab work using cold-pressor tests, so the two claims should not be merged.

The ISO principleB

Music therapy's oldest working rule, set out by Ira Altshuler in 1948: match the person's current state first, then move it gradually. You do not play calm music at an agitated person. You meet them, then lead.

This is entrainment used on purpose, and it is a decent model for how presence works between people too. Meet the state, then shift it.

Style and identityB

Taste consolidates during adolescence, which is when identity forms and exposure peaks, though as in section 05 the exact window is disputed. Genre works as a tribe marker.

"I'm into this right now" often means "this is the state I am in, or the state I want to be in". Same song, different week, different landing. The song did not change.

ScriptureS

David and Saul, 1 Samuel 16, with the caution in section 04 attached.

The Psalms are the Bible's own playlist, covering the full emotional range, including the songs you would rather not sing.

Paul and Silas singing in prison at midnight, with the other prisoners listening (Acts 16:25).

Music in scripture is mostly corporate and mostly response. It is what people do when something has already happened.

Where it breaksC

Tuning-frequency claims do not carry the weight put on them. On 432 against 440 hertz there is one small double-blind crossover pilot, Calamassi and Pomponi in 2019, thirty-three participants, which found a heart rate about five beats per minute lower at 432. That is a real but tiny effect in a small study, and a separate performance study pointed the other way. It is nowhere near the health claims made for it.

The Solfeggio frequencies have a worse footing still. They trace to Joseph Puleo in the 1970s, derived by numerology from the Book of Numbers, and expanded later by Leonard Horowitz. They are not connected to Guido of Arezzo's actual medieval syllables, which were never tied to a frequency at all, because hertz did not exist as a unit until the nineteenth century.

Intention in worship matters, and is defensible theologically. Hertz as medicine is not.

08

People

People sync when they feel safe with each other, and safety comes from predictability, non-judgment, and somebody else's calm. Everyone runs on a different clock, and that is the design rather than the bug. Connection is attunement, not synchronisation.

Co-regulationB

Nervous systems regulate each other. This is best established between infants and caregivers and reasonably supported in adults: a calm, present person measurably lowers arousal in an anxious one.

That is what "safe people" means physiologically. It is also why one anxious person can tilt a whole room.

Synchrony and likingB

Moving in time together, walking, singing, rowing, increases cooperation and liking. Scott Wiltermuth and Chip Heath's 2009 experiments are the usual citation, and they need an asterisk: a direct replication of the third study found no effect, and the meta-analyses since report a real but small effect that depends heavily on context. So the direction holds and the magnitude does not.

Similarity attracts, but so does complementarity. The best pairs are not the identical ones. They are the mutually readable ones.

The different-clocks problemC

One person has just eaten and wants to run. The other has just run and wants to eat. One wants to go deep tonight, the other has nothing left. Most relational friction is timing, not incompatibility.

The temptation is to go looking for people on your clock. Paul's body metaphor says the opposite: different parts, different timings, and the work is attunement (1 Corinthians 12:12-27). Ecclesiastes 3 says there is a time for everything. It does not say everyone is in the same time.

Which is also what the two pendulums at the top of the page settle into. Not the same swing. A stable relationship between two different ones.

Safe people, described plainlyC

Predictable. Not keeping score. Regulated more often than not. Able to disagree without it becoming a threat. Not needing you to be in their state.

The last one is the hard one, and it is the difference between sharing your resonance and imposing it.

Scripture

"Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you." John 14:27. Peace as something transferred. And "if possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all" (Romans 12:18): peace as something you bring to the table, not something you require from it.

Where it breaksC

The wish for everyone to resonate with your thing is mimetic desire plus the need for shared reality, because an experience feels more real when somebody else confirms it. It is the same root as evangelism, and it is also how the people around you end up performing agreement out of fear.

If you need other people in your state in order to feel safe, you are in the closed loop.

09

Alignment

Alignment is when your state, your attention, your desire, your people and your actions agree. The Bible's word for it is closer to shalom than to "everything going smoothly": things in right relation, including the hard things. And the entry point is not managing all five. It is the one that opens the others.

The one-sentence versionC

Trust produces safety. Safety produces peace. Peace opens attention. Open attention produces resonance. Resonance shapes desire and taste. Shared safety produces connection. Connection produces fruit. Fruit confirms trust.

Fear runs the same chain backwards.

Psychology's versionB

Kennon Sheldon and Andrew Elliot's self-concordance model, 1999: goals that match your own values get more sustained effort and produce more wellbeing than goals adopted under pressure. Widely replicated.

Carl Rogers' congruence says distress comes from the gap between the self you present and the self you experience. Cognitive dissonance is the felt cost of the same gap.

Scripture's versionS

"Unite my heart to fear your name." Psalm 86:11. A prayer for an undivided heart.

"If therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light." Matthew 6:22, KJV. The ESV has "healthy" rather than "single", which is a real difference in the Greek and worth knowing about.

The double-minded man is "unstable in all his ways" (James 1:8). "Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life" (Proverbs 4:23): state upstream of everything.

And Psalm 23, which has both "he restores my soul" and "you prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies". That is alignment with the threat still standing there. Shalom is not the absence of enemies. It is a set table.

The simple practiceC

Not: audit your vibe. Not: find the frequency. Not: make everything confirm.

Instead: notice which loop you are in, and the body will tell you. Narrowed, braced, chasing, keeping score. Do the bottom-up things first: sleep, food, light, movement, a safe person. Then return to the Word expecting it to be alive, because it already is. Let resonance be data. Test it outside yourself. Hold it loosely enough that the people around you do not have to perform it.

Where it breaksC

Alignment is not a promise that nothing goes wrong. Paul's thorn. Gethsemane. Job.

The aligned life in scripture includes friction, unanswered prayers, and being out of step with the room. What it does not include is the closed loop as a permanent address.

Reading the marks

Every claim on this page carries one of four. The point is that you can always see which kind of thing you are looking at.

A

Measured. Replicated findings with named researchers. You can look these up.

B

Supported, qualified. Real evidence, but contested in detail, or the popular version overstates it.

C

Inferred. Our synthesis. This is where the page connects things the research does not connect on its own. Treat as hypothesis.

S

Scripture. Quoted or closely paraphrased, reference given.

What this page is, and what it isn't

  • It is an attempt to put one explanation next to another and label which is which.
  • It is not a health claim, a diagnosis, or a technique. Nothing here is a method for producing a state.
  • Where the research is thin, the page says so in the drawer rather than quietly rounding up.
  • Several claims were downgraded while this was being written, because the source turned out to be smaller, or more contested, than the popular version of it. Those downgrades are visible in the chips.
  • The two loops are a model. Real people are in both in the same afternoon.
