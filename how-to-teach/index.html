The whole engineSummary

Engineer reconstruction.
Keep it slightly effortful.
Spread it across time.

Three variables predict whether learning persists: whether the learner reconstructs the material rather than rereading it, whether that reconstruction requires effort, and whether it is distributed across time rather than massed.

Nearly every robust finding below is a special case of that sentence. The rest of this page is the sentence, unpacked — and then enacted on you.Most of the findings below are instances of one of those three variables.

The eight moves

01

🔁Retrieval, not reviewTest yourself instead of rereading

🟢Robust

Pulling knowledge out strengthens it; putting it back in — rereading — barely moves the needle. The test is not the measurement of learning. It is the learning.Recalling information strengthens memory for it more than rereading does. A test changes memory rather than only measuring it. So self-quizzing is not a way to check whether studying worked — it is the studying.

📊 Evidence
Rowland 2014: g = 0.50 across 159 comparisons. Adesope 2017: g ≈ 0.61–0.70. Dunlosky rates practice testing high utility — one of only two techniques to receive that grade.
⚠️ Where it stops
Not universal. A 2025 meta-analysis of mathematics learning found the effect at g = 0.18 with a confidence interval crossing zero.Not universal. A 2025 meta-analysis of mathematics learning found testing versus restudy at g = 0.18, with a confidence interval crossing zero. The effect varies by domain.
02

⏳Space itSpread practice across days

🟢Robust

The same total effort spread across days beats the same effort crammed. Let it decay a little, then force the harder rebuild.The same total study time produces more retention when distributed across sessions than when massed. Delay permits partial forgetting, which makes later recall harder and therefore more effective.

📊 Evidence
Distributed practice is the other technique Dunlosky rates high utility. Benefits appear across ages, materials, and retention intervals.
⚠️ Where it stops
The optimal gap scales with how long you need to remember it. There's no single correct interval.The optimal gap scales with the intended retention interval. There is no single correct spacing schedule.
03

🔀InterleaveMix problem types

🟡Moderate

Mixing problem types instead of drilling one blocks the pattern-matching shortcut and forces the learner to first decide which tool applies. That decision is most of the skill.Mixing problem types requires the learner to identify which method applies before applying it. Blocked practice removes that step, because the context supplies the method.

📊 Evidence
Dunlosky rates interleaved practice moderate — real benefits, but at the time of review systematic exploration had only begun.
⚠️ Where it stops
Weaker and more domain-dependent than retrieval and spacing. Don't file it in the same drawer.Weaker and more domain-dependent than retrieval and spacing. It does not carry equal weight.
04

🪜Scaffold, then kick the scaffold awayMatch guidance to prior knowledge

🟢Robust

For a true novice, a clean worked example beats making them flail. Once the schema exists, that same example robs them, and generation becomes the thing that teaches.Novices learn more from worked examples than from unguided problem solving, because they lack the schema needed to reconstruct a solution. Once the schema exists, the same guidance stops helping and can interfere.

📊 Evidence
The expertise reversal effect (Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller). Guidance that helps novices can actively harm more advanced learners.
⚠️ Where it stops
Usually a partial reversal — support stops helping — rather than a full flip to harm.Usually a partial reversal, in which support stops helping, rather than a full reversal in which it harms.
05

🗣️Make them explainHave learners explain

🟡Moderate

Self-explanation and elaborative interrogation force the learner to build the causal links themselves instead of receiving them prebuilt.Prompting learners to state why something is true, in their own words, produces better outcomes than supplying the explanation. The learner generates the causal links rather than reading them.

📊 Evidence
Both rated moderate utility by Dunlosky. Promising; under-tested in real educational settings rather than labs.
⚠️ Where it stops
Moderate means moderate. The evidence base is thinner than for retrieval and spacing.The evidence base is thinner than for retrieval and spacing.
06

🕳️Mind the gap you can't seeExperts skip steps without noticing

🟡Moderate

As expertise grows you silently compress the steps you no longer notice, and the learner falls straight into the gap.Experts omit steps that have become automatic and are no longer available to introspection. The steps that feel most obvious to the teacher are the ones most likely to be skipped, and where learners most often get stuck.

📊 Evidence
The curse of knowledge is well-documented as a bias. Its specific instructional cost is more inferred than directly quantified.
⚠️ Where it stops
I'm rating this moderate on purpose. The bias is real; the effect size on teaching outcomes is not something I found a clean number for.Rated moderate deliberately. The bias is documented; I did not find a clean effect size for its cost to teaching outcomes.
07

😬Distrust the smilePerceived learning is unreliable

🟢Robust

Students taught actively learned measurably more — and reported feeling like they'd learned less. Effort gets misread as failure.Students in active classrooms scored higher and reported lower perceived learning than students given polished lectures on identical material. Learners interpret cognitive effort as evidence they are learning badly.

📊 Evidence
Deslauriers et al. 2019 (PNAS), randomized, identical materials, experienced and highly-rated lecturers on the passive side.
⚠️ Where it stops
Implication: positive reception is not evidence your teaching worked. It may be mild evidence against.Implication: positive reception is not evidence that teaching worked, and may be weak evidence against it.
08

🚨Build something that can say noInclude a way for the learner to be wrong

🟡Moderate

Retrieval and teaching only work when something can genuinely fail the learner. Without a real no, they rehearse whatever model they walked in with.Practice without corrective feedback rehearses whatever model the learner already holds, including an incorrect one. Repetition increases confidence in a wrong model as readily as a correct one.

📊 Evidence
Feedback strengthens retrieval practice (Rowland). Tutoring's advantage is generally attributed to immediate error correction and adaptive pacing.
⚠️ Where it stops
The sharpened version — that a falsifying loop is the load-bearing element — is my synthesis, not a finding.The claim that this is the single most important element is my inference, not a finding. Treat it as a testable design principle.
What to stop repeating

Four claims that are weaker than they sound.

Two are overstated. Two are actively disputed.

🎨Matching instruction to learning style

🔴Overreach

The testable claim is the meshing hypothesis: that matching instruction to a preferred style improves outcomes. Validating it requires a crossover interaction. Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer and Bjork found that very few studies used a design capable of testing this, and several that did produced contradicting results.

🧭 The honest version
A 2024 meta-analysis reports that roughly a quarter of the outcome measures it examined did show the required crossover pattern. This does not support the practice, but the question is less closed than most debunkings state.

🖍️Rereading and highlighting

🔴Overreach

Both received low utility ratings from Dunlosky, alongside summarization, the keyword mnemonic, and imagery for text. They were included in the review because students rely on them heavily.

🧭 The honest version
They are not harmful. They increase familiarity with the text without increasing recall, and familiarity is easily mistaken for knowledge.

📈Growth mindset interventions

🟠Contested

Sisk et al. found an average effect on achievement of about d = 0.08. A 2025 structured review reports d = 0.05 overall and d = 0.02 when restricted to the highest-quality studies. Macnamara and Burgoyne attribute apparent effects to design and reporting problems.

🧭 The honest version
Defenders argue the meta-analyses averaged real effects in predicted subgroups, such as low-achieving students, together with null effects where no effect was hypothesized, and that pre-registered large trials did find gains in the predicted subgroup. The dispute is unresolved.

🎯Bloom's two sigma

🟠Contested

The claim that one-to-one tutoring moves the average student two standard deviations. It has not been replicated at that magnitude. VanLehn's systematic review found the next-largest effect near 0.82; a replication of one source study produced about 0.52. Tutoring meta-analyses cluster around 0.36–0.37 SD.

🧭 The honest version
Tutoring works. The specific figure is not supported. Bloom framed the paper as an open challenge rather than a result.

Side by side

The same lesson, taught two ways.

Topic: why distributed practice beats cramming.

❌ Transmission

Distributed practice is more effective than massed practice. When study sessions are separated by intervals, partial forgetting occurs between them, and the effort of re-retrieval strengthens memory more than a fluent repetition would. This is called the spacing effect.

Accurate. Clear. Well written. The reader nods and retains almost nothing, because they did no work.
✅ Reconstruction

Two students study four hours each. Ana does all four the night before. Ben does one hour on four separate nights. Who scores higher on a test two weeks later?

Most people say Ben, and they're right. So here is the harder one, and the reason this version teaches: who scores higher the next morning?

Often Ana. Massing can win on an immediate test. That is exactly why cramming survives — rewarded on the timescale students are graded on, punished on the timescale they need. If you had only read the paragraph above, you would not have that.

Worked example · a craving loop
⚠︎ EXAMPLE CONTENT — a stand-in, to demonstrate the six steps. The transferable thing is the procedure, not these claims.

Teaching the loop, and the exit, without explaining either first.

STEP 1🎬

A concrete case

Something feels empty. Something promises to fill it. You reach, and for a moment the reaching stops. Then the emptiness is back — a little deeper — and the same something promises again.A person feels a lack. A behavior promises to remove it. They perform the behavior and the urge subsides briefly. The lack returns, somewhat stronger. The same behavior promises again. Across repetitions, more is required to produce the same relief.
STEP 2✋

Predict: where would you intervene?

The explanation stays hidden until you answer.

STEP 3⚡

Why that answer does or doesn't work

The pursuit. The most commonly chosen option and the least effective. It intervenes after the motivational state is already active, so it requires sustained effort against it.
Removing triggers reduces frequency. But triggers are numerous and substitutable, so this addresses instances rather than the mechanism.
This is the earliest intervention point, and the one the mechanism below supports. Note that arriving at it yourself makes the explanation easier to retain than reading it would have been.
Restricting access to the reward still intervenes after the motivational state has formed, so it faces the same difficulty as resisting the pursuit.

The most effective intervention point is the earliest one — when the prediction forms — not the point of strongest urge.

STEP 4💡

The mechanism

Wanting is the motivational pull toward a stimulus. Liking is the hedonic satisfaction produced by consuming it. The behavior reduces wanting without producing liking, so the underlying lack persists. Wanting also adapts upward with repetition.

Resisting the pursuit therefore addresses a downstream symptom. The upstream cause is the prediction that this behavior will produce liking, which it structurally cannot.

STEP 5🔁

Reconstruct it from memory

State the stages, and why resisting the pursuit fails. Don't scroll back.

Lack → prediction that a behavior will remove it → the behavior → wanting subsides, liking is not produced → the lack returns, stronger → repeat, with escalation.

Resisting the pursuit fails because it intervenes downstream of the prediction that drives the loop.

✅ Correct.
Hunger is a homeostatic cycle. Eating produces liking — you are genuinely satisfied — and it does not escalate: tomorrow's hunger is not stronger than today's.
The model has two discriminators, and you used both: does it produce liking? and does it escalate?
❌ Not this one.
The option you chose does fit: it reduces wanting, produces no liking, and escalates. The exception is hunger. Eating produces genuine satisfaction, and hunger does not escalate across days.
The discriminators are does it produce liking? and does it escalate? Getting this wrong locates where your model was underspecified.
Worked example · a statistical idea

Survivorship bias, taught in four minutes.

Watch how much of the work happens before any explanation arrives.

STEP 1🎬

A concrete case

It is 1943. Bombers return from raids over Europe riddled with holes. Analysts map where the damage falls: heavily on the wings and rear fuselage, hardly at all on the engines and cockpit. Armor is heavy. You can only reinforce some of the plane.
STEP 2✋

Predict: where do you put the armor?

STEP 3⚡

Why that answer does or doesn't work

The most common answer, and precisely backwards. Before reading on: ask yourself which planes were available to be examined.
Correct, and unusual. Hold it — but make sure you can say why, because the right answer for the wrong reason will not transfer.
Spreading armor evenly wastes weight on regions that demonstrably tolerate damage. It also avoids the question rather than answering it.
A defensible engineering tradeoff, but it sidesteps the inference problem. The data on the table is still being read incorrectly.

One question dissolves this: which planes were available to be examined?

STEP 4💡

The mechanism

Only the planes that came back could be measured. The holes therefore mark the places a bomber can be hit and still return. The clean areas are not lucky — they are where hits were fatal, and those aircraft are at the bottom of the Channel.

Armor the engines and cockpit. The statistician Abraham Wald, working for the Statistical Research Group at Columbia, developed the method for estimating vulnerability from damage to survivors.

The general form: when your sample is selected on the outcome you are studying, the pattern in the data is inverted.

⚠️ Wald's wartime memoranda are real. The widely circulated diagram of a bomber covered in red dots is a modern illustration, not his, and the anecdote is often retold with embellishment. The statistical point stands; the story around it has been polished.
STEP 6🧪

Apply it to a case that could break it

Three of these are survivorship bias. One is not. Which one?

✅ Correct.
Random assignment means the sample was not selected on the outcome. The other three all condition on survival: dropouts who failed aren't famous, badly built old buildings were demolished, and cats that died on impact were never carried into the vet's office.
The discriminator: was the sample selected on the outcome being studied?
❌ Not this one.
The case you picked does condition on survival. The exception is the randomized trial: random assignment means the sample was not selected on the outcome.
The discriminator: was the sample selected on the outcome being studied? Notice you can now state the rule, which you could not have done from the definition alone.
Worked example · a theological idea

The fear of God, taught without defining it first.

⚠︎ MY CONSTRUCTION. The direction-of-movement reading is a homiletical framework, not a settled exegetical result. Some scholars argue the Hebrew does not cleanly separate dread from reverence, and that the numinous includes real terror. Testable, not authoritative.
STEP 1🎬

Two concrete cases

A man hears God moving in the garden and hides himself among the trees. Asked why, he says he was afraid.
Another sees the Lord high and lifted up, cries out that he is ruined, unclean, undone — and does not move. A coal from the altar touches his mouth. He stays. He volunteers.

Genesis 3:8–10 · Isaiah 6:1–8. Both men are terrified. Both texts use fear language.

STEP 2✋

Predict: which man fears God?

STEP 3⚡

Why that answer does or doesn't work

The intuitive answer, and it treats intensity as the criterion. Adam is certainly afraid. But notice what his fear does to him: it moves him away from God and into the trees.
You've located it. But be careful you can say why — Isaiah is not less afraid than Adam. He is undone. The difference is not the volume of the fear.
Close, and the most defensible of the four. Hold the question of whether a fear that reliably drives you away and a fear that reliably draws you in are usefully called the same thing.
Too quick. The word is doing real work in both texts. The question is whether it is doing the same work.

The discriminator is not how much fear. It is which direction the fear moves you.

STEP 4💡

The mechanism

The Hebrew root yare' carries both senses — dread and reverence — and the single English word inherits the ambiguity. The term does two jobs, and the reader silently picks one.

Adam's fear is centrifugal: it moves him out of the presence. Isaiah's is centripetal: it holds him in the presence he cannot survive, and he is cleansed there rather than elsewhere. Same terror. Opposite vector.

On this reading, the fear of God commended in the wisdom literature is not a lower dose of terror. It is terror reoriented toward its object rather than away.

STEP 6🧪

Apply it to a case that could break it

Three of these move away from God. One moves toward. Which?

✅ Correct — now here is where the model strains.
Peter, in Luke 5:8, falls at Jesus's knees and says depart from me, I am a sinful man. His words move away. His body moves toward. A clean direction test does not resolve him.
That is not a defect in the exercise. It is the exercise. A framework that never meets a case it cannot sort has not been tested — and if you build teaching on this distinction, Peter is the case you owe an answer to.
❌ Not this one.
The case you chose moves away from the presence. Isaiah is the exception: undone, and unmoving.
Now the harder case, which the model does not cleanly sort: Peter falls at Jesus's knees and says depart from me. Words away, body toward. Where does he go? You can only feel the force of that because you committed to a rule first.
The hardest case

How Jesus taught — and where it breaks this page.

⚠︎ READ FIRST. What follows is retrofitting. Reading twenty-first-century cognitive science onto a first-century rabbi is anachronistic by construction. The gospels are theological documents, not transcripts. The parable was a standard Jewish teaching form, so nothing here claims originality of method. And this section has no ornamented variant — the prose toggle does nothing below — because this is precisely the ground on which the temptation to sound profound is strongest.

One counter, often quoted: Copenhaver tallies 307 questions asked by Jesus, 183 asked of him, and 3 he answers directly. Reviewers dispute the count — several point to places he does answer plainly, and Copenhaver elsewhere allows fewer than ten. The figure depends entirely on an undefined term: answers directly. Take the direction of it, not the number.

❓Luke 10:25–37

He answers a question with a question — then inverts it

A lawyer asks who counts as his neighbor. Jesus does not define the term. He tells a story, then asks the lawyer which of the three men acted as a neighbor. The lawyer produces the answer himself. And the question has been reversed: he asked who qualifies for his love; he leaves with the question of whom he is a neighbor to.

↳ Maps to
Steps 2, 3 and 6. The commit is forced. The reframe is the friction. Go and do likewise is transfer.
🪞2 Samuel 12:1–7

The listener convicts himself first

Not Jesus, but the purest specimen of the form, and he would have known it well. Nathan tells David a story about a rich man who takes a poor man's only lamb. David, enraged, passes sentence. Then Nathan applies it: you are the man. David committed to the verdict before he knew it was his own.

↳ Maps to
A commit so complete the learner cannot retreat. The reveal lands as self-recognition rather than information.
🪙Mark 12:13–17

He makes them produce the evidence

Asked a trap question about taxes, he does not answer. He asks for a coin, then asks whose image is on it. They answer. Only then does he speak, and what he says depends on the words they just said.

↳ Maps to
Generation, not transmission. He requires an act and an utterance from the learner before supplying anything.
🗣️Mark 8:27–33

Forced commitment, then an immediate transfer test

He asks what people say about him, then narrows it: who do you say that I am? Peter commits. He is right. Moments later Jesus explains that the Messiah must suffer and die, Peter objects, and is rebuked sharply. Peter had the correct label attached to the wrong model.

↳ Maps to
Step 6, unmistakably. A right answer at step 2 does not guarantee the model survives a new case.
🚶Luke 10:1–20

Practice, then debrief

He sends seventy-two out to do the work before they have seen it done to completion. They return and report, and he responds to what they bring back — including correcting what they were pleased about.

↳ Maps to
Deliberate practice with feedback. He does not lecture them into readiness first.
🐫Mark 10:25

Ornament as memory technology

A camel through the eye of a needle. A plank in the eye. Salt that loses its taste. In an oral culture with no notebooks, hyperbole and vivid absurdity are not decoration — they are how a saying survives transmission at all. This complicates the strip pass below, and it should.

↳ Maps to
The distinction that holds: his images are welded to hard, specific, costly claims. The vividness makes a definite thing memorable. It does not stand in place of the thing.
Where the model breaks

Four places he does not do what this page recommends.

If every example confirmed the framework, the framework would be doing no work.

🚪He often omits the reveal entirely

Most parables end without explanation. The disciples get private interpretations occasionally; the crowd frequently does not. On this page's model, that is a broken lesson — step 4 never arrives. On his, it appears to be the point.

🌑He describes the parables as concealing

Asked why he speaks in parables, he says the secret is given to those around him but that outsiders receive parables so that seeing they do not perceive, quoting Isaiah 6. Mark uses a purpose construction (hina); Matthew shifts to a causal one (hoti), which reads more like consequence than intent. The interpretation is heavily contested. What is not contested is that no modern instructional designer would write this.

🥀He lets people leave

After a hard teaching in John 6, many disciples stop following. He does not soften it or chase them. The rich young ruler walks away and is not pursued. A method optimized for comprehension does not do this. A method optimized for allegiance might.

🎯The objective function is different

This page optimizes for retention and transfer of a model. He is not primarily trying to install a model. Measuring him by test scores is a category error, and the fact that his methods happen to look like good pedagogy is worth noticing but proves nothing.

What he has that this page doesn't

A parable withholds the reveal on purpose. The hearer carries it around, unresolved, for days or years. It surfaces again at a wedding, at a graveside, when a Samaritan stops on a road. It is retrieval practice with no scheduler, no app and no notification — the story does its own spacing, by refusing to close.

Which is exactly the technique this page cannot perform. Retrieval happens here in one sitting; spacing does not happen at all. The oldest teaching method in the room is built entirely on the pillar the newest one is missing.

🛑 And the honest limit
That paragraph is the most attractive idea on this page, which is the reason to distrust it. It is my inference, unmeasured. Nobody has tested whether an unresolved parable produces durable retention, and the mechanism I have named — delayed re-retrieval — is a modern category imposed on the material. It also risks reducing a call to repentance to a memory technique, which is the specific way this section could be wrong while feeling true.

The honest caution

The same emotional spike that makes a true insight stick makes a wrong-but-compelling one feel just as true. Vividness and accuracy are separable.Emotionally vivid ideas are remembered well whether or not they are correct. Vividness does not track accuracy, so the confidence an idea produces is not evidence for it.

So the last move isn't the one that feels best. It's the one most able to tell you you're wrong. Build that in — for your learners, and for yourself.So choose the method most able to disconfirm you, rather than the one that feels most persuasive. Build that in for your learners and for yourself.
