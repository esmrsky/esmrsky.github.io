God’s heart when love is refused

They have not rejected you.They have rejected me.

God to Samuel, the day Israel asked for a king, 1 Samuel 8:7

Pursue or Wait
When to go after someone, and when to keep watch

If you have ever poured yourself into someone and watched them walk away to something that hurts them, you have felt a small piece of what God has felt for all of history.

This page looks at rejection from God’s side and from the side of anyone who leads, parents or loves like him: what it does to his heart, why people run to counterfeits, and how Scripture shows four different ways love goes after the lost.

1Rejection is God’s story first

No one has been walked away from more.

From the garden to the cross, the Bible is the story of a God who keeps reaching for people who keep leaving. Twelve moments.

  1. The garden

    The first humans hide. God comes looking anyway, calling “Where are you?”

    Genesis 3:8-9

  2. The golden calf

    While Moses is on the mountain for forty days, the people who just heard God’s voice trade him for a statue they can see.

    Exodus 32:1

  3. The king

    They want a king like everyone else. God tells Samuel who they are really rejecting.

    1 Samuel 8:7

  4. The prophets

    He sent messengers “again and again, because he had pity.” They mocked them.

    2 Chronicles 36:15-16

  5. The cisterns

    Two sins at once: leaving the spring, then digging broken wells that hold no water.

    Jeremiah 2:13

  6. Hosea’s wife

    God tells Hosea to marry a woman who will be unfaithful, then to go and love her again, so Israel can see what it is doing to God.

    Hosea 1:2, Hosea 3:1

  7. His own

    “He came to that which was his own, but his own did not receive him.”

    John 1:11

  8. Jerusalem

    “How often I have longed to gather your children... and you were not willing.”

    Luke 13:34

  9. The crowd leaves

    After a hard teaching, many disciples walk away. Jesus turns to the twelve: “You do not want to leave too, do you?”

    John 6:66-67

  10. His closest friend

    Peter swears he never knew him. Jesus had already prayed for him.

    Luke 22:31-32

  11. Despised

    “Despised and rejected by mankind, a man of suffering, and familiar with pain.”

    Isaiah 53:3

  12. He wept

    Coming over the hill toward the city that would kill him, he cried over it.

    Luke 19:41-42
=

The same sentence, a thousand years apart. When people turn away from someone carrying God’s heart to them, the rejection travels past that person to God. That is not a license to call every disagreement “rejecting God.” It is a comfort: when you are refused for loving well, it is not only about you, and you are not alone in it.

2What it does to God

He is not indifferent. He is not controlling. He is hurt.

Scripture does not describe a God who shrugs when people leave. It uses the language of a parent and a spouse.

Grief

Before the flood, the Bible says God’s heart was deeply troubled. Paul warns that the Spirit can be grieved.

Longing

A hen spreading her wings over chicks that won’t come. The verb is “longed.” The refusal is theirs.

Jealousy

Not insecurity. The fierce love of a husband who will not share his bride with what is destroying her.

Turmoil

One of the most emotional things God says in the Old Testament. Justice says let them go. Love can’t.

Not controlling

God does not drag people back by force. “You were not willing” means their will was real, and he honored it, even weeping.

Not indifferent

He also never stops caring. Whatever line he draws, he draws it with tears, not with a shrug.

3Other lovers

Why people run to things that can’t hold water.

The people God pursued rarely left for nothing. They left for something that promised what only he could give. Hosea calls these “lovers.” Jeremiah calls them broken cisterns.

Broken cisterns

A spring is free and alive. A cistern is work, and it leaks. People trade the spring for the thing they can control. (There is a whole site on this: The Broken Cistern.)

Asking the wrong god

King Ahaziah was hurt and sent to ask a foreign idol whether he’d recover. God’s question through Elijah: “Is it because there is no God in Israel?”

A voice when God is quiet

When God stopped answering Saul, he didn’t repent. He went to a medium. Counterfeits are often a shortcut around a silence.

Going back to Egypt

Israel kept looking to Egypt for protection, the very place God had rescued them from (Isaiah 30:1-2). Old slavery feels safer than new trust.

If it feels like being cheated on, you’re not wrong about the feeling.

Spiritual fathers, mothers, pastors and friends know this pain: you gave your time and your heart, and they went to a counterfeit that is hurting them. God uses exactly that language about himself.

Hosea gives the whole picture. She runs after her lovers, can’t catch them, and finally says the most honest line in the book:

Notice who built the thorn hedge. God doesn’t only wait for the counterfeit to fail. Sometimes he makes sure it does.

4Four kinds of lost

Love doesn’t do the same thing for everyone who is lost.

In Luke 15 Jesus tells three stories in a row, and the one who loves acts differently in each. Hosea adds a fourth. The question is not “should I pursue?” It is “what kind of lost is this?”

Pursue

Go after it and carry it home.

A sheep doesn’t decide to leave. It nibbles its way off, one patch of grass at a time, until it can’t find its way back. It isn’t rebelling. It’s lost and probably scared.

Who this looks like

A young believer who drifted. Someone overwhelmed by grief, shame or a crisis who stopped showing up and doesn’t know how to come back.

What love does

Leaves the comfortable ninety-nine, goes out, finds them, and carries them. On its shoulders, rejoicing, not lecturing.

The trap

Treating a lost sheep like a rebel son. Waiting for someone to “come to their senses” when they don’t know the way.

Search

Light a lamp. Sweep. Search carefully.

The coin never left the house. It got lost inside, rolled into a dark corner, while everyone walked past it. It doesn’t even know it’s lost.

Who this looks like

Someone in the third row every Sunday who no one really knows. A child in a Christian home going through the motions. Present, and lost.

What love does

Turns the lights on and looks carefully, in its own house. Asks real questions. Notices who has gone quiet.

The trap

Assuming that present means found. Being so busy with the house that no one sweeps it.

Wait, watch, then run

Let him go. Keep watching. Run when he turns.

The son chose. He asked for his share and left with it. Notice what the father doesn’t do: he doesn’t follow him to the far country, send servants after him, or cut off the road home.

Who this looks like

An adult who knows the truth and has decided to walk away from it. They know exactly where home is.

What love does

Lets the famine teach what lectures can’t (Luke 15:14-17). Keeps the door open, the robe ready, and eyes on the road. He saw his son “while he was still a long way off.” You only see someone that far away if you have been looking.

When he turns

Run. Interrupt the apology. Throw the party. And go out to the older brother too, who is lost in a different way (Luke 15:28-32).

Two traps

Chasing someone who has chosen, which turns love into control. Or shutting the door, which turns hurt into pride.

Hedge, woo, redeem

Block the road to the counterfeits. Speak tenderly. Buy her back.

The bride is different from the son. She is bound by covenant, and she keeps going back to other lovers. God’s response in Hosea has three movements, and none of them is force.

Who this looks like

God’s own people, who belong to him and keep running to idols. In human terms, someone you are joined to by covenant or real responsibility.

What love does

Hedges (prays that the counterfeits stop working, Hosea 2:6). Woos (leads into the wilderness and speaks tenderly, 2:14). Redeems (pays the price to bring her home, 3:2).

The trap

Trying to do God’s part. Only God can build the hedge and only he can win a heart. Our part is to pray and to keep a door of hope open.

Hosea is a picture of God’s love for his people. It is not a rule that anyone must stay in an abusive or unsafe relationship. Scripture also protects the vulnerable, and getting safe is not a failure of love.

Which kind of lost is this?

Think of one person. Answer four questions. It points you to a story, not a verdict. Ask God about it too.

Did they knowingly choose to leave?
Do they know the way back?
Are they still showing up, still around?
Are you bound to them by covenant or real responsibility?

Start with the first question.

Most people are a mix. The answer shows which story to read first.

5The lines love draws

God pursues. God never forces. And sometimes he says stop.

These are hard to hold together, and the Bible holds them anyway. Every one of them is God’s own way of loving someone who refuses him.

He knocks. He doesn’t break in.

The risen Jesus stands at the door of a church that has left him outside. He knocks and speaks. The handle is on their side.

Revelation 3:20

He let the rich man walk away.

“Jesus looked at him and loved him,” and still did not run after him or lower the cost.

Mark 10:21-22

He gives time.

Even to a false teacher leading people astray: “I have given her time to repent.” Patience is a form of pursuit.

Revelation 2:21

He gives people over.

The most sobering line in Romans: when people insist, God hands them over to what they chose. It is the son’s far country, on a larger scale.

Romans 1:24

“How long will you mourn?”

Samuel grieved over Saul after God had rejected Saul as king. God told him to stop mourning and go anoint David. There is a time to move on to what God is doing next.

1 Samuel 16:1

No fire from heaven.

A village refused Jesus. James and John wanted to call down fire. Jesus rebuked them and went to another village.

Luke 9:54-56

Shake off the dust.

When a town wouldn’t listen, the disciples were to leave without carrying it with them. Not bitterness. Just not dragging it along.

Matthew 10:14

The watchman’s part ends.

A watchman must warn. If the warning is refused, he is not responsible for their choice. Your part is to speak, not to make them listen.

Ezekiel 3:19

God grants repentance. You don’t.

Paul tells Timothy to be gentle and patient “in the hope that God will grant them repentance.” One plants, one waters, God gives the growth.

2 Timothy 2:24-26, 1 Corinthians 3:6-7
Never stop praying.

Even when Israel rejected him, Samuel said failing to pray for them would be a sin (1 Samuel 12:23). Whatever else changes, this doesn’t.

Never stop leaving the door open.

The father’s house always had a road home and someone watching it. Letting go of the chase is not closing the door.

6For spiritual fathers and mothers

Paul knew this pain, and so did Jesus.

If you lead, disciple, parent or mentor, you will be refused. Here is how the people closest to God’s heart carried it.

Paul, open-hearted

He wrote to a church that had pulled away from him and asked them, plainly, to open up again.

Paul, spending himself

The question every pursuer eventually asks.

Paul, in labor

He uses a mother’s image for discipling, and admits he doesn’t know what to do.

Jesus and Peter

Jesus knew the denial was coming. He prayed ahead of time, and after the resurrection he went to find Peter on the beach and restored him three times.

Paul and Mark

Paul refused to take Mark on a second trip after Mark had deserted them (Acts 15:37-39). Years later: “Get Mark and bring him with you, because he is helpful to me.” Some rejections heal.

Before you chase, and before you let go

Five questions to keep your own heart clean while you love someone who keeps leaving.

  1. Is this about me?“They have rejected me,” God said. Your wound is real, but it isn’t the center (1 Samuel 8:7).
  2. Am I trying to be their savior?You plant and water. God gives the growth (1 Corinthians 3:6-7).
  3. Have I said what love needs to say?Clearly, gently, once more than feels comfortable (Ezekiel 3:19).
  4. What kind of lost is this?Sheep, coin, son or bride. Each asks something different of you.
  5. What is God telling me?Go after them, keep watch, or “how long will you mourn?” Ask him. He knows them better (1 Samuel 16:1).
The door of hope

The father was watching the road.

The Valley of Achor means “the valley of trouble.” God promises to turn the place where everything went wrong into a door. That is his heart for everyone who has walked away, and the heart he gives to anyone who loves like him: go after the sheep, search for the coin, watch for the son, fight for the bride, and never stop praying.
