A word study — Greek · Hebrew · Slavonic · Russian

κρίνωJudgment

Μὴ κρίνετε. Loading…Matthew 7:1 · The only line anyone quotes
τὴν δικαίαν κρίσιν κρίνατε. Loading…John 7:24 · Same verb. Now an order.

The contradiction isn’t in the Bible. It was made in English, by one word doing the work of twenty-eight.

The short version

Five things, and then you’re done.

01

Judging is sorting

The Greek word means to sift — grain from chaff, on a threshing floor. You did it at the fruit crate this week and didn’t call it judgment.

02

English lost the prefix

Greek had twenty-eight words for this. δια- means apart. κατα- means down-against. The prefix carried the entire moral difference. English has one syllable: judge.

03

Matthew 7 tells you to do it

One warning about method — then six instructions you cannot obey without sorting. Including “by their fruits you will know them,” fifteen verses later.

04

Six things are fenced off

Motives. Where someone finally stands. Appearances. Preferences. Outsiders. Anything you haven’t heard yet. That is the actual list — and none of them is “deciding whether a thing is good.”

05

And it means rescue

In Hebrew, to judge is to put something right. The Judges of Israel saved people. That’s why the psalm has trees singing when the Judge arrives.

The same act, named three ways

Three languages. Three pictures.

Greek, Latin and Slavic each had to name the act of judging, and each reached for a different everyday scene to name it with. Two reached for the kitchen and the market. One reached for a courtroom.

There is a hard proof of the Greek picture, and it isn’t from a lexicon. Latin cernō — “to sift, to discern” — descends from the very same root as κρίνω. From it Latin built crībrum, a literal sieve, and also certus (certain), sēcernō (secret — what is sifted apart) and crīmen (crime). From the Greek side: crisis, critic, criterion. To be certain is to have finished sifting. A crisis is the moment the sorting happens.

Sixty seconds, no theology

Pick the apple you’d take home.

κρίνω krinō — to separate, to sift, to decide

You held six things against a standard you never said out loud, and acted on the result. That verb is the one in Matthew 7:1.

What the sources turned up

Three things that landed harder than expected.

Every text on this page was pulled from a primary edition rather than recalled — Tischendorf for the Greek, the Westminster Leningrad Codex for the Hebrew, and Синодальный, НРП and the Church Slavonic Bible for the Russian. Three findings came out of that:

One sentence, forbidden and commanded

Romans 14:13 uses κρίνω twice — “Let us no longer judge one another, but rather judge this.” In English that is a riddle. It is the single strongest verse on this page.

A translator solved it in the 9th century

The Church Slavonic renders that same pair ѡ҆сꙋжда́емъ (osuzhdaem — condemn) then сꙋди́те (sudite — judge) — using the prefix English lacks. It does the same at 1 Cor 12:10 and Titus 3:11.

A judge is a rescuer

Judges 2:16 states the judges’ defining act with יָשַׁע, to save. Which is why Psalm 96 has trees singing because the Judge is coming.

And the “people are lying to themselves” problem has a Greek word too: πρόκριμα — a verdict reached before the hearing 1 Tim 5:21. Built identically to Russian предрассудок and Latin prae-iūdicium. Meanwhile Matthew 7 opens with “judge not” and, fifteen verses later, hands you a fruit-inspection procedure. One warning, six orders to sort, zero bans.

05 The six fences What you genuinely may not judge — and the four things you are ordered to.

Scripture fences off particular objects of judgment and leaves the rest wide open. Once you see the fences, the contradiction dissolves.

Fence 01

Motives you cannot see

Not the act — the reason behind it. Paul refuses to do this even to himself.

μὴ πρὸ καιροῦ τι κρίνετε…“Judge nothing before the time, until the Lord comes, who will bring to light what is hidden.” 1 Cor 4:5
Fence 02

Where someone finally stands

You may say “this is wrong.” You may not say “and therefore you are lost.”

By appearance

The oldest and most consistent prohibition in Scripture — and the one modern life breaks most often.

Food, days, custom. Romans 14 forbids judgment exactly where Scripture has not spoken — and where religious people judge most confidently.

06 The Hebrew floor Underneath the Greek: to judge is to put something right. The Judges were rescuers.

The apostles thought in Hebrew and wrote in Greek. κρίνω is the word the Greek Old Testament already used to translate שָׁפַט — and the Hebrew idea is not a courtroom. It is a repair.

שָׁפַט
shāphaṭ · H8199
To set right. To govern. To vindicate.

Used of a ruler who intervenes for someone who cannot get justice alone. The emphasis falls on the outcome, not the pronouncement.

Septuagint renders it κρίνω.
מִשְׁפָּט
mishpāṭ · H4941
The right ordering of things

Not “a ruling” but justice as a state of affairs: what each person is owed, actually delivered. This is what Micah says God requires.

Septuagint renders it κρίσις and κρίμα.
דִּין
dîn · H1777
To plead someone’s case

To take up a cause for a person who has none. The verb points at the victim: you judge for the poor, not against the rich.

Septuagint renders it κρίνω.

The Judges were not magistrates

The book called Judges is שֹׁפְטִים — shophetim. Gideon, Deborah, Samson. Not one presides over a trial.

וַיָּקֶם יְהוָה שֹׁפְטִים וַיּוֹשִׁיעוּם
“The LORD raised up judges, and they saved them.” Judges 2:16

The verb for saved is יָשַׁע, the root behind the name Yeshua. In the sentence that defines a judge, the job is rescue.

Which is why creation sings about it

If judgment were fundamentally a sentence, Psalm 96 would be unreadable. Instead the Judge’s arrival is the climax of a song:

“Let the heavens be glad… let the trees of the forest sing for joy before the LORD, for he comes to judge the earth.” Psalm 96:11–13

Nobody throws a party for a sentencing. They throw one when the person arriving is going to fix it.

One of the nine gifts of the Spirit is a judging faculty, listed between prophecy and tongues without apology. A church that decided judging is unspiritual has quietly deleted an item from its own list.

Рассуждение — the greatest virtue

The Eastern ascetic tradition never lost the distinction, because its working language never lost the prefix. In Cassian’s record of the desert fathers, διάκρισις is argued to be the mother of the virtues — the one that keeps every other from tipping into excess. Zeal without it burns people.

Осуждение — the characteristic sin

In the same tradition, condemning another person is named again and again as the besetting danger — precisely because it counterfeits the virtue. It feels like discernment and uses the same evidence. It arrives at a person instead of a fact, and it exempts the one holding it.

08 “Don’t judge” is a verdict The part that stings — and the reason Scripture regulates judging instead of banning it.

Say it out loud and listen to the grammar. It is a ruling, about a person, reached in seconds, usually without hearing the case — by someone standing outside the standard they just applied. Greek has a word for that: πρόκριμα.

Paul saw it coming

ἐν ᾧ γὰρ κρίνεις τὸν ἕτερον, σεαυτὸν κατακρίνεις, τὰ γὰρ αὐτὰ πράσσεις.

“In judging another you condemn yourself, because you, the judge, do the same things.” Romans 2:1

Read the reason Paul actually gives. Not because judging is wrong — because you do the same things. Remove that clause and the argument collapses. Most popular readings remove exactly that clause.

One ordinary day, honestly

You judged the weather and took a coat. You judged a route, a price, a headline, a restaurant’s cleanliness, a stranger on the platform. You judged a friend’s new partner inside four minutes.

Then you said — sincerely — “I try not to judge people.” That is not humility. It is an inaccurate description of a life, and the inaccuracy does work: it keeps the judgments running while removing them from review.

09 Nine things that went missing None of it is hidden knowledge. It went missing because one English word had to carry nine Greek ones.

Judgment is a sieve before it is a gavel

Try “sort” wherever you read “judge,” and watch how many contradictions evaporate.

Latin cernō, from the same root, gave crībrum — a sieve — and also certain, discern, secret and crime.

Matthew 7 is a manual, not a ban

One warning about method, six instructions requiring discrimination, zero prohibitions on assessment.

καὶ τότε διαβλέψεις ἐκβαλεῖν τὸ κάρφος — “and then you will see clearly to remove the speck.” Matt 7:5

The final judgment is a light switched on

John describes illumination arriving and people sorting themselves by what they do with it. The verdict is not imposed on the scene — it is the scene.

αὕτη δέ ἐστιν ἡ κρίσις, ὅτι τὸ φῶς ἐλήλυθεν — “And this is the judgment: the light has come.” John 3:19

Discernment is a trained muscle

The mature have faculties gymnasium-trained by practice — which means the ability is built by judging, badly, repeatedly, and being corrected. A blanket ban prevents exactly that.

τὰ αἰσθητήρια γεγυμνασμένα Heb 5:14

Self-judgment cancels judgment

The one court where you are both defendant and judge is the court that keeps you out of the other one. Stated as a mechanism, not a piety.

You are being trained for a bench

In Hebrew, to judge is to rescue

The Judges saved people. Dîn means to plead the case of someone who has none. Jeremiah identifies doing it with knowing God.

וַיָּקֶם יְהוָה שֹׁפְטִים וַיּוֹשִׁיעוּם — “The LORD raised up judges, and they saved them.” Judges 2:16

Refusing to judge is half of an abomination

Proverbs names both failures in one line and gives them the same verdict. Letting the guilty walk is on the ledger, in the same column, as condemning the innocent.

The word for the bad judge is a judging word

ὑποκριτής — one who answers from under a mask. Jesus does not accuse the man of judging. He accuses him of performing a judgment he has exempted himself from. The charge is theatre.

11 What the brain is actually doing You cannot obey “do not judge” as a ban. The machinery runs in a tenth of a second, before you are consulted.

Cognitive science arrived at the same shape as the Greek, from the other end. Judgment is not an optional act you perform on people you dislike. It is the brain’s default operation, running continuously, mostly below awareness.

01 · Speed

A tenth of a second

Willis and Todorov showed faces for 100 milliseconds and asked for trait ratings. The judgments matched those of people given unlimited time — and the strongest correlation of all was trustworthiness.

Extra viewing time did not change the verdict. It only increased confidence in it.

Willis & Todorov, “First Impressions: Making Up Your Mind After a 100-Ms Exposure to a Face,” Psychological Science 17 (2006), 592–598. This is why a ban cannot work. By the time you decide not to judge someone, you already have. What is left to choose is what you do with the verdict.
02 · The plank, measured

You cannot see your own bias

Pronin, Lin and Ross asked people to rate how susceptible they were to a list of biases, compared with others. People rated themselves as markedly less biased than the average person, than classmates, than fellow travellers.

And when the mechanism was explained to them, they insisted their own self-assessment was the accurate one anyway.

Pronin, Lin & Ross, “The Bias Blind Spot: Perceptions of Bias in Self Versus Others,” Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 28 (2002), 369–381. Matthew 7:3 in a lab. The plank is not hidden by dishonesty — it is hidden by the geometry of looking out of your own eyes. Which is why the instruction is to remove it first, not to try harder to see past it.
03 · The criterion

The threshold has a name, and it is the same word

Signal detection theory splits any judgment call into two independent quantities: sensitivity — how well you can actually tell signal from noise — and the criterion, the threshold you set for calling something a signal. Move the criterion and you trade one kind of error for the other. You never remove both.

Proverbs 17:15 names the same two errors and calls both an abomination — “acquitting the guilty and condemning the innocent.” Refusing to judge does not escape the grid. It just parks you in the bottom row, where the misses live. The technical term for that threshold, borrowed into psychophysics from Greek, is criterion — κριτήριον, the instrument of judging.
04 · Two speeds

Fast judgment, slow judgment

The fast system categorises automatically and cheaply — it is what fires at 100ms. The slow one is deliberate, effortful, and lazy: it usually ratifies whatever the fast one produced rather than auditing it.

“Judge with righteous judgment” John 7:24 is an instruction to put the second system on the case — not to pretend the first one is off.
05 · Prediction

Prejudice is a prior

On predictive accounts of perception, the brain does not receive the world and then evaluate it. It runs a model, predicts the input, and processes mainly the error. What you already expect therefore shapes what you perceive — before any conscious weighing happens.

Which is exactly πρόκριμα, a verdict filed before the hearing. A prior is not a sin; an unexamined prior is. “Test everything” is the instruction to check the model against the error.

Put together: the brain judges constantly, fast, and invisibly; it cannot audit itself from the inside; and every threshold it sets trades one error against another. A blanket ban on judging is not humility — it is an instruction the hardware cannot execute. What Scripture asks for instead is the only thing that is actually available: check the measure, clear your own eye first, and know which errors you are choosing.

Every one of those five is a restatement of something already on this page. That is not because the Bible anticipated cognitive science. It is because both are describing the same animal.

12 Sources, and what is contested Which texts these quotations come from, and the two places the argument could be pushed back on.

Greek text

Tischendorf’s Novum Testamentum Graece, 8th ed. (1869–72), with Strong’s numbering. Where it prints συνκρίνω, later critical editions print συγκρίνω; the word list uses the standard lemma spelling.

Hebrew text

Westminster Leningrad Codex, pointed, with Strong’s. Proverbs is cited by Hebrew versification, which differs from the Synodal numbering in chapters 17–18 — so no Synodal text is quoted for those verses.

Russian & Slavonic

Синодальный перевод; Новый русский перевод (НРП); Church Slavonic Bible of 1900. The Slavonic is quoted because it is the oldest surviving witness in this family to how the prefix problem was solved.

Septuagint

The Hebrew-to-Greek mappings — שָׁפַט → κρίνω, מִשְׁפָּט → κρίσις/κρίμα, שֹׁפְטִים → κριταί — come from the lemmatised Septuagint at the verses cited, not from a secondary list. That text is lemmatised, so it is never quoted here as running prose.

Etymology

PIE *krey- “to sift” for both κρίνω and Latin cernō; Proto-Slavic *sǫdъ from *som-dʰh₁-os, “that which is placed together”; ὑποκριτής from ὑποκρίνομαι, with the Attic sense stage actor.

What is contested

Two things. Whether Matthew 7:1 forbids condemnation specifically or censorious judging more broadly — Luke’s parallel is strong evidence, not proof, and careful commentators differ. And сосуд: it is built on the same *sǫdъ element, though not every dictionary treats “vessel” and “court” as one word. Neither point changes the argument, which rests on the prefixes.

Nothing here licenses being harsh. The same letters that command discernment command it in a spirit of gentleness, aiming at restoration, by people who have looked in a mirror first Gal 6:1. The argument is not that Christians should judge more — it is that they already do, constantly, and pretending otherwise is what makes the judging bad.
