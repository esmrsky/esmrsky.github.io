Psalm 90:2

Before the mountains were born

Is God inside time, walking the road with us, grieving, relenting, answering? Or outside it, seeing the whole road at once, never surprised and never changed? Both answers read the same Bible. This site puts all of it side by side and lets the texts argue.

The inside-time answer

A Father who lives the story with us

God has no beginning and no end, but his life runs forward, moment after moment. He knows everything that can be known, and much of the future is settled by his plans and promises. Some of it waits on what people choose. So when the Bible says God regretted, relented, tested, or said “now I know,” it means it.

Leans on Genesis 6:6, Exodus 32:14, 1 Samuel 15:11, Jeremiah 18:7-10, Jonah 3:10, Genesis 22:12 and the father running down the road in Luke 15.

Where it strains. “I am the LORD, I do not change” and “declaring the end from the beginning” have to be read as faithfulness and purpose, not as sight of every future choice.

The outside-of-time answer

A God who holds the whole at once

Time is something God made. He has no before and after in his own life, so he never waits, remembers or anticipates. He knows every moment, the way an author holds a whole book. When the Bible says he relents or grieves, it describes how his unchanging holiness looks from our side of a changing story.

Leans on Psalm 90:2, Malachi 3:6, James 1:17, Numbers 23:19, Isaiah 46:9-10, Psalm 139:16, Ephesians 1:4 and Revelation 13:8.

Where it strains. A question like “Where are you?” or a sentence like “it did not enter my mind” has to mean less than it says, and a timeless God has to act, speak and answer in time.

Between the two, a whole range

Most Christians in history have stood somewhere along this line. The positions differ on two questions: does God live through time, and how much of the future does he know or fix?

  1. Timeless and decreeingGod is outside time and has decreed all that happens. Augustine, Aquinas, Calvin, Edwards.
  2. Timeless and seeingGod sees every free choice in one eternal present, without causing it. Boethius, many Arminians.
  3. Middle knowledgeGod knows what every person would freely do in any situation, and chooses the world to make. Molina, William Lane Craig.
  4. Everlasting and foreknowingGod lives through time with no start or end, and still knows the whole future. Wesley, Swinburne in part, many evangelicals.
  5. Open and relationalGod lives in time and knows the future as partly settled, partly open. Greg Boyd, Clark Pinnock, John Sanders; Father-Son theology sits here.
  6. ProcessGod is changed by the world and does not rule it by power at all. Whitehead, Hartshorne. Both sides above reject it.

Six texts the whole question turns on

The verse follows the translation picker at the top. Try The Passion Translation on Jeremiah 18 or the King James on Exodus 32 to see how much a single word carries.

Both sides claim it

1 Samuel 15:29 he is not a man that he should change his mind

He who is the Glory of Israel does not lie or change his mind; for he is not a human being, that he should change his mind.”

NIV

The tension is the whole debate in one chapter. The common solution: verse 29 means God will not reverse the specific decision to reject Saul, while verses 11 and 35 describe sorrow.

Both sides claim it

Malachi 3:6 I do not change

“I the Lord do not change. So you, the descendants of Jacob, are not destroyed.

NIV

Immutability of character and promise, which both sides accept. It says nothing on whether God experiences time. Classical readers use it for “God does not change at all.” Others say it is about loyalty.

Leans inside time

Jeremiah 18:7-10 the potter’s rule for nations

If at any time I announce that a nation or kingdom is to be uprooted, torn down and destroyed, and if that nation I warned repents of its evil, then I will relent and not inflict on it the disaster I had planned. And if at another time I announce that a nation or kingdom is to be built up and planted, and if it does evil in my sight and does not obey me, then I will reconsider the good I had intended to do for it.

NIV

Pro-conditionality and inside time. Classical readers say all prophecy carries unstated conditions. Open-view readers say this is the actual way God governs.

Leans outside time

Isaiah 46:9-10 declaring the end from the beginning

Remember the former things, those of long ago; I am God, and there is no other; I am God, and there is none like me. I make known the end from the beginning, from ancient times, what is still to come. I say, ‘My purpose will stand, and I will do all that I please.’

NIV

Strong for real foreknowledge, and for prophecy as the mark of the true God. It does not say how God knows (outside time, or from within it).

Leans inside time

Exodus 32:9-14 Moses pleads; the Lord relents

“I have seen these people,” the Lord said to Moses, “and they are a stiff-necked people. Now leave me alone so that my anger may burn against them and that I may destroy them. Then I will make you into a great nation.” But Moses sought the favor of the Lord his God. “ Lord ,” he said, “why should your anger burn against your people, whom you brought out of Egypt with great power and a mighty hand? Why should the Egyptians say, ‘It was with evil intent that he brought them out, to kill them in the mountains and to wipe them off the face of the earth’? Turn from your fierce anger; relent and do not bring disaster on your people. Remember your servants Abraham, Isaac and Israel, to whom you swore by your own self: ‘I will make your descendants as numerous as the stars in the sky and I will give your descendants all this land I promised them, and it will be their inheritance forever.’ ” Then the Lord relented and did not bring on his people the disaster he had threatened.

NIV

Strongly inside time and interactive: a prayer comes, and what God does next changes. The classical reply: God had an unstated condition, or this is how Scripture speaks of God’s response.

Leans outside time

Psalm 139:16 all the days ordained for me were written

Your eyes saw my unformed body; all the days ordained for me were written in your book before one of them came to be.

NIV

If “days,” it supports strong foreknowledge or a planned life (and an outside-time view of the whole). If “members,” it is about God’s care in forming a person.

Before the Mountains sets the two answers side by side and lets the texts argue. It does not pick a winner. A companion to A Father Inside Time and its Cross-Examination.
